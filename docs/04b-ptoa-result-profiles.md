# PTA Result Profiles

> Companion to `04-passage-of-time-assessment.md`.
> Full-depth profiles for each PTA result, using the same 14-section schema as Tests 1, 2, and 3 so all four tests are directly comparable.
> These are the deterministic fallback narratives. The Gemini layer enriches the Archetype and Reflection sections from the player's answer pattern. It never overrides the structured sections.
> Honesty note: profiles describe tendencies associated with a combination of two measured dimensions, temporal direction and temporal mode. They are affirming, non-clinical, and avoid deficit framing. Every past-anchored result is described as rootedness, never as being stuck. Every fluid result is described as freedom, never as incoherence. Every present-oriented boundary result is described as living in the now, never as a gap.

---

## Canonical schema (every type)

1. **Essence.** One-line core plus the two axis scores.
2. **The Archetype.** Two short narrative paragraphs.
3. **Core Motivations.** What drives this mode.
4. **Behavioral Patterns.** Observable tendencies.
5. **Strengths.**
6. **Blind Spots.** Honest, non-shaming.
7. **Growth Edges.** Specific development.
8. **In Relationships.** What they offer, need, and risk.
9. **Work Style.** Ideal conditions, contributions, pitfalls.
10. **Decision-Making.** Tendencies and traps.
11. **Under Stress.** Stress signature and recovery.
12. **Communication.** Style and growth.
13. **The Complementary Mode.** The opposite quadrant they can learn from.
14. **Your Reflection.** The poetic closing line about moving through time. AI-personalized in-game.

## 📜 The Archivist  (Past + Continuous)

**Essence.** You hold your roots as one stable thread and your life as a single, ordered story. You preserve, and you keep.

### The Archetype

You move through time backward-facing and steady. Where you have been matters as much as where you are going, and you carry it all as one continuous thread. Your memories are not decoration; they are the ground you stand on. You know your roots, you keep what matters, and you order your life so the past is not lost to the present.

You experience your life as a single, coherent story. The person you were years ago is unmistakably still you, grown. The chapters connect. When you make a choice, you weigh how it fits the larger arc you have been living, and you trust the throughline. The steadiness runs deep: you are not reinvented by every season, you are continued by it. People come to you for context, for the long view, for the sense that something endures.

### Core Motivations
- To preserve what matters: people, memory, meaning, lineage.
- To live a coherent life, one where the past, present, and future belong together.
- To honor continuity: with yourself, your family, your place.
- To keep faith with where you have been.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You hold onto objects, photos, letters, places that carry meaning.
- You mark anniversaries, traditions, and rituals, and you keep them going.
- You tell stories from your past often, and they have a clear shape.
- You are slow to abandon commitments: relationships, projects, places.
- When you face a choice, you ask how it fits with who you have always been.

### Strengths
- Steadiness and depth. You are the same person across the years.
- Loyalty and fidelity. You keep your word and your people.
- A long view. You see patterns and context others miss.
- Wisdom drawn from memory. You carry forward what was learned.
- The ability to hold a group, a family, a project together across time.

### Blind Spots
- Holding on past the point of usefulness. Some things ask to be released.
- Reading the future through the past and missing what is genuinely new.
- Underestimating people who remake themselves freely; their fluidity can look like shallowness to you.
- Equating change with loss.
- A tendency to curate the story until it is tidier than the living of it was.

### Growth Edges
- Let some chapters close. Release is not betrayal.
- Make room for the unfamiliar. The story is allowed to take a turn you did not plan.
- Meet the Pioneer's comfort with reinvention. Coherence does not require stasis.

### In Relationships
- You offer loyalty, memory, and the feeling of being held across time.
- Partners know where they stand with you; you do not rewrite the past of a bond.
- You may hold on to relationships that have run their course, out of fidelity to who you were together.
- Growth: let people change within the relationship. Continuity can include becoming.

### Work Style
- Thrive in roles requiring stewardship, depth, continuity: archives, history, conservation, long-term research, family business, mentoring, restoration.
- You shine at keeping knowledge and culture alive across people and time.
- Risk: resisting necessary change. The institution you preserve must also grow or it dies.
- Ideal: environments that value depth and the long arc, where patience is rewarded.

### Decision-Making
- Guided by precedent, by what has worked, by who you have been.
- You decide slowly and rarely regret it, because the choice fits the larger arc.
- Risk: declining good new options because they break pattern.
- Strength: your decisions have the weight of accumulated wisdom behind them.

### Under Stress
- Signature: retreat into the past. You seek the familiar, the known, the preserved.
- You may idealize how things were and resist the present.
- Recovery: return to the throughline, but ask what the next chapter needs, not only what the last one had.
- Watch: preservation hardening into refusal. You can keep something alive by refusing to let it change, or kill it the same way.

### Communication
- Measured, contextual, story-rich. You frame the present by the past.
- Growth: bring the conversation forward sometimes. Not everything needs to be rooted.
- Your memory is a gift. Pair it with curiosity about what has not happened yet, and it becomes foresight.

### The Complementary Mode: 🌱 The Pioneer
Your wholeness lives in what you have not yet become. The Pioneer's comfort with reinvention, with letting the self reshape as the future unfolds, is the motion your steadiness needs. Continuity without renewal calcifies. The Pioneer shows you that the story you keep can also be the story you are still writing.

### Your Reflection
"You are the keeper of the thread, the one who remembers and who stays. Your gift is continuity, and your growth is letting the story surprise you."

## 🧭 The Navigator  (Future + Continuous)

**Essence.** You chart one continuous course forward, carrying a coherent sense of where you are headed. You plan, and you pursue.

### The Archetype

You move through time forward-facing and steady. The horizon pulls you, and you know where you are going. Your life has direction, and you carry yourself toward it as one continuous being: the person you are becoming is already legible in who you are now. You are not drift. You are trajectory.

What sets you apart is that your future orientation is not restless. You are pulled forward, but the pull is grounded in a coherent sense of self and story. You can describe where you are headed and how this choice fits the larger arc. You plan, you pursue, and across the years you become who you intended to be. You make the future legible, for yourself and for others.

### Core Motivations
- To build something real across time.
- To live on purpose, with direction and a coherent arc.
- To become the person you are aiming at.
- To make the future happen rather than wait for it.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You set goals and work toward them over years.
- You think about where choices will lead before you make them.
- You keep your sense of self stable even as you grow toward your aims.
- You finish what you start.
- Others experience you as directed, reliable, and going somewhere.

### Strengths
- Vision and follow-through. You close the distance between now and next.
- Coherence. Your actions and your identity align across time.
- Resilience grounded in direction. You can endure present difficulty for a future aim.
- The ability to make plans real.
- Trustworthiness on long horizons. People invest in you because you deliver across years.

### Blind Spots
- Over-planning. Some of the best things cannot be scheduled.
- Reading your future so tightly that you miss what the present offers.
- Mistrusting people who pivot freely; their fluidity can look like flakiness to you.
- Confusing your plan with your purpose. The plan can change without the purpose dying.
- Letting the horizon shrink the present to a waiting room.

### Growth Edges
- Leave room to be surprised. The story can take a turn that serves you more than the plan.
- Let the present be a place to live, not only a step on the way.
- Meet the Storyteller's looseness with the past. Not everything has to be carried forward as freight.

### In Relationships
- You offer direction, commitment, and the sense that the relationship is going somewhere.
- Partners trust your follow-through over the long arc.
- You may be so oriented toward what you are building that you under-attend to what is here now.
- Growth: be present in the relationship, not only future-oriented with it.

### Work Style
- Thrive in roles requiring long-horizon planning and execution: strategy, leadership, research programs, building a company or body of work, medicine, engineering.
- You shine at turning vision into delivered results across years.
- Risk: rigidity. When the plan no longer serves, you can hold it past the point of cost.
- Ideal: environments that reward direction, patience, and the long arc.

### Decision-Making
- Future-weighted and coherent. You decide by where the choice leads and how it fits the arc.
- You decide deliberately and usually well.
- Risk: declining good options that do not fit the plan, even when the plan is stale.
- Strength: your decisions compound. Today's choice builds on years of aligned ones.

### Under Stress
- Signature: grip the plan harder. You tighten the timeline, refine the route, push forward.
- You may lose the present entirely under pressure.
- Recovery: return to purpose, then ask whether the plan still serves it.
- Watch: direction turning into compulsion. You can be headed somewhere real and still miss the life happening on the way.

### Communication
- Clear, forward-looking, structured. You speak in aims, steps, outcomes.
- Growth: make room for what is felt and uncertain. Not everything worth saying has a destination.
- Your direction is a gift. Pair it with presence, and it becomes leadership rather than propulsion.

### The Complementary Mode: 🗣️ The Storyteller
Your wholeness lives in what you can release and retell. The Storyteller's looseness with the past, the willingness to hold memory as material to be remade, is the flexibility your direction needs. A course held too tightly becomes a rut. The Storyteller shows you that coherence can include revision, and that some of the best arrivals were never on the map.

### Your Reflection
"You are the one who knows where you are going and carries yourself there as one. Your gift is direction, and your growth is arriving without losing the journey."

## 🗣️ The Storyteller  (Past + Fluid)

**Essence.** You weave the past into a living, revisable present, holding memory loosely and retelling as you grow. You remember, and you remake.

### The Archetype

You move through time backward-facing and fluid. The past is alive for you, but you do not keep it under glass. You carry it as material, retelling it, reworking it, finding new meaning in old scenes as the years change you. Memory is not a fixed archive in you; it is a living story you are always telling again.

Your sense of self shifts across the years, and you are honest about that. The person you were is real, but no longer quite you, and that is not a failure. It is how you grow. You hold the past without being held by it. Where The Archivist preserves one continuous thread, you weave and reweave, letting each season retell the last. A particular freedom comes with this: you are not bound to remain who you were, even as you honor where you have been.

### Core Motivations
- To keep the past meaningful without being trapped by it.
- To grow, which for you includes revising how you tell your own story.
- To hold memory as living material, not as fixed record.
- To honor where you have been by letting it keep speaking in new ways.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You retell old stories often, and the telling shifts with who you have become.
- You reconnect with your roots in waves rather than continuously.
- You change how you see your past as you change.
- You value memory but resist being defined by it.
- Others find you reflective, layered, and hard to pin down across the years.

### Strengths
- Meaning-making. You find new significance in old experience.
- Freedom from a fixed story. You are not sentenced to who you were.
- Emotional depth drawn from a rich, revisable past.
- The ability to integrate hard history by retelling it toward growth.
- Honest self-revision. You do not pretend you were always who you are.

### Blind Spots
- Rewriting the past until the truth of it blurs. Revision can slide into revisionism.
- Losing the throughline. A self retold too often can lose its spine.
- Others may feel the ground shift under them: which version of you are they getting?
- Mistaking a good retelling for real change.
- Distance from the present. Living to retell can crowd living.

### Growth Edges
- Hold the core steady even as the telling shifts. Some things should not be reworked.
- Let the present be more than material for a future story.
- Meet the Navigator's forward direction. The past is richest when it feeds somewhere you are going.

### In Relationships
- You offer depth, reflection, and the sense that your shared history keeps yielding new meaning.
- Partners may find it hard to know which you is the real you, because you shift.
- You need people who can grow with you and tolerate revision.
- Growth: offer some constancy. Love benefits from a self that does not entirely rewrite between seasons.

### Work Style
- Thrive in roles requiring reflection, narrative, and integration: writing, therapy, teaching, ministry, research that synthesizes, any work that draws meaning from experience.
- You shine at helping people and institutions reframe their past toward growth.
- Risk: circling the past without moving forward. Reflection without direction can stall.
- Ideal: environments that value meaning and honest revision, and that do not demand a fixed persona.

### Decision-Making
- Past-informed and revisable. You decide by what your history has taught, and you allow yourself to revise.
- You are open to changing course when the story demands it.
- Risk: indecision born of too many possible retellings.
- Strength: your decisions carry the weight of reflection without the rigidity of fixed precedent.

### Under Stress
- Signature: retell harder. You revise the story over and over until the versions blur and the throughline thins.
- You may lose contact with the present and the future under pressure.
- Recovery: ask not only what the past means but what the next chapter needs.
- Watch: revision turning to circling. Note: the PTA does not measure rumination (bitter, stuck recall of the past), which is the unmeasured shadow on this pole. If circling tips into that, this result cannot speak to it.

### Communication
- Narrative, reflective, layered. You speak in stories and in what they have come to mean.
- Growth: arrive at the point sometimes. Meaning does not require a full arc.
- Your reflection is a gift. Pair it with forward motion, and it becomes wisdom rather than reverie.

### The Complementary Mode: 🧭 The Navigator
Your wholeness lives in where you are going. The Navigator's forward direction and coherent arc is the spine your reflection needs. Memory without trajectory circles. The Navigator shows you that the past is richest when it feeds a future you are actually moving toward, and that some of your best stories will be about where you arrived, not only what you survived.

### Your Reflection
"You are the one who remembers and remakes, who keeps the past alive by letting it change. Your gift is meaning-making, and your growth is letting the story lead somewhere."

## 🌱 The Pioneer  (Future + Fluid)

**Essence.** You move into the new on many emerging threads, comfortable letting the self reshape as the future unfolds. You explore, and you become.

### The Archetype

You move through time forward-facing and fluid. The future calls, and you answer it on many fronts at once, willing to become someone you cannot yet name. You are not bound to a single plan or a single self. You are drawn to what is emerging, what is possible, what has not been tried, and you trust yourself to reshape as you go. For you the past is closed context, not living material: it explains how you arrived, not where you are going.

This is not restlessness, though it can look like it. It is a genuine relationship with the unknown as a place to live. You experience your self as in-motion: the person you will be is genuinely open, and that openness is where you do your best work. Where The Navigator charts one course, you launch several and see which holds. You are at home in beginnings, in possibility, in the self that is still becoming. Others feel the current of you: always a little ahead, always a little in-flux, always alive to what is next.

### Core Motivations
- To move toward what is emerging and possible.
- To become, freely, without being sentenced to who you have been.
- To explore many threads and see what holds.
- To live at the frontier of your own life.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You start new things often and may have several running at once.
- You are comfortable with your sense of self shifting as you grow.
- You are drawn to the new: people, places, fields, projects, selves.
- You leave chapters behind more easily than you keep them.
- Others experience you as forward, alive, and sometimes hard to keep up with.

### Strengths
- Initiative and exploration. You open doors others did not see.
- Adaptive selfhood. You are not trapped by who you were.
- Comfort with uncertainty. You can act before the path is clear.
- Renewal. You rebuild yourself after change and keep going.
- Vision that is genuinely open, not pinned to one outcome.

### Blind Spots
- Leaving things unfinished. Beginning is your gift; completion can suffer.
- Losing the thread of who you are across too many pivots.
- Others may experience you as inconsistent or hard to rely on over the long arc.
- Underestimating the value of the past and of continuity; roots can look like anchors to you.
- Confusing motion with progress.

### Growth Edges
- Finish something. Follow one thread all the way and see who you become within commitment.
- Carry a throughline. You can be open and still continuous.
- Meet the Archivist's rootedness. The past is not only ballast; it is ground.

### In Relationships
- You offer aliveness, possibility, and the sense that the relationship is always opening.
- Partners may fear you will move on, in role or in self, before they are ready.
- You need people who can grow with you and who do not require a fixed you.
- Growth: offer some staying. Love benefits from a self that persists across the seasons, not only one that opens.

### Work Style
- Thrive in roles requiring initiative, exploration, and adaptability: founding, R&D, creative fields, frontier research, anything early-stage, cross-disciplinary work.
- You shine at opening new ground and at rebuilding yourself for what a field demands.
- Risk: leaving a trail of beginnings without the completed work that compounds.
- Ideal: environments that reward initiative and adaptability and that tolerate a self in motion.

### Decision-Making
- Possibility-weighted and adaptive. You decide by what opens the most future, and you revise freely.
- You can pivot fast when new information comes.
- Risk: abandoning good bets too early because something newer appeared.
- Strength: you rarely stay stuck in a decision that no longer fits.

### Under Stress
- Signature: launch more threads. Open more options, move faster, become someone who can handle it.
- You may fragment across too many fronts under pressure.
- Recovery: choose one thread and follow it for a while. Continuity is medicine for you here.
- Watch: motion becoming flight. You can outrun real problems by reframing them as next chapters.

### Communication
- Forward, open, generative. You speak in possibility and in what could be.
- Growth: close the loop sometimes. Report what landed, not only what is launching.
- Your initiative is a gift. Pair it with follow-through, and it becomes creation rather than motion.

### The Complementary Mode: 📜 The Archivist
Your wholeness lives in what endures. The Archivist's rootedness and continuity is the ground your motion needs. Possibility without ground scatters. The Archivist shows you that a self can be open and still held, and that some of the deepest exploration happens within a life you have chosen to keep.

### Your Reflection
"You are the one who moves into the new and is willing to be reshaped by it. Your gift is becoming, and your growth is letting some of who you are stay."

## 🌅 The Horizon  (the boundary condition)

**Essence.** You live near the present edge where past and future meet. You are not strongly pulled backward or forward. You are here.

### The Archetype

You are not anchored in the past and you are not chasing the future. You live where they meet, which is now. This can show up in two ways, and the facet signature tells them apart.

Some of you arrive here genuinely balanced: you hold your roots and your horizons in proportion, neither dwelling nor racing, and the present is where you do your living. Your time is whole, not divided.

Others arrive here present-focused: neither the past nor the future pulls you hard, because the only time you fully inhabit is this one. The literature calls this present-orientation, and it is not a deficit. It can be the active, eudaimonic presence of someone fully engaged with what is in front of them. The PTA does not measure that presence as a validated pole (the dominant time-perspective instrument has no positive-present factor), so it reads you as the boundary, affirming what you are rather than claiming more than the construct supports.

When facet tension is present (you are strongly engaged with **both** past and future at once, or your continuity and coherence scores pull in opposite directions), the modifier fires. You are not moderate; you are near center because strong forces are holding each other or filling the whole of time. The narrative then leads with the facet signature: which facet drove which axis, and what that means for how you move through time. Note: a present-focused respondent (low on both past and future engagement) also lands near center, but that reads as the affirming Horizon, not as tension.

### Core Motivations
- To live fully in the present, neither leaning too hard on what was nor on what will be.
- (If balanced) to hold the whole of time in proportion.
- (If present-focused) to inhabit the only moment that is actually here.

### Strengths
- Presence. You are not elsewhere.
- Proportion (if balanced). You see past, present, and future in their right size.
- Aliveness to what is actually happening now.

### Blind Spots
- Missing long arcs because the present is so vivid.
- (If present-focused) under-preparing for the future or under-honoring the past.
- Drift, if presence becomes avoidance of the demands of direction or memory.

### Growth Edges
- Visit the past and the future on purpose sometimes. Presence is richer when it includes them consciously rather than by default.
- Name which you are: balanced, or present-focused. They point to different growth.

### The Complementary Modes
- If balanced, learn from all four modes without needing to become any one of them.
- If present-focused, the Navigator offers forward direction and the Archivist offers roots, each a pole to visit and return from.

### Your Reflection
"You are the one who lives where the past and the future resolve into now. Your gift is presence, and your growth is choosing to visit time's other rooms and bringing their light back here."

## How scores modulate these profiles

The base profile above is the quadrant archetype. A player's exact position on the plane modulates it.

| Signal | Meaning | Effect on the profile |
|---|---|---|
| High prototypicality (far from center) | Strongly that mode | Report the archetype at full strength. |
| Low prototypicality (near center), no facet tension | Genuinely balanced or present-focused | Report **The Horizon** boundary. Pull one or two lines from each adjacent mode. |
| Low prototypicality (near center), with facet tension | Near center from cancellation, not balance | Same Horizon boundary, but with the facet-tension modifier narrative. Lead with the facet signature. |
| Mode result, with facet tension on one axis | A mode pulled in two directions internally | Report the mode as normal, then surface the facet-tension modifier as a secondary note led by the facet signature. |
| Near an axis boundary | Strong on one axis, neutral on the other | Emphasize the dominant axis. Soften the secondary. |
| Angle maps to early, mid, or late | Position along the quadrant arc | Shift tone. "Early Pioneer" is just starting to explore. "Late Storyteller" is nearing the Archivist's steadiness. |

This is the hybrid dimensional and prototypical structure (Kerber et al. 2021). A memorable type plus honest gradation. The opposite of MBTI's cliff at the midpoint.

## Facet reporting (core, not optional)

The PTA is facet-organized. Every result reports the two axis scores and their four facet sub-scores. The facet signature lets a profile distinguish *which facet drove the axis score*.

- Axis 1, Temporal Direction: A1 Past Engagement, A2 Future Engagement.
- Axis 2, Temporal Mode: B1 Temporal Self-Continuity, B2 Narrative Coherence.

A past-anchored result can now read "anchored in the past through strong past engagement, with little pull toward the future." A continuous result can read "continuous mostly through self-continuity, less through narrative coherence." This is the depth that separates the PTA from a flat axis quiz. A1, A2, B1, and B2 are theory-grounded facets of validated or designer-adapted constructs, not independently validated factors; that line is stated on the results screen.
