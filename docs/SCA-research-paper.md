# The Solstice Cycle Assessment

## A personality framework built on how you burn, how you move, and where you land on the cycle of light

---

The summer solstice is the longest day of the year. The sun climbs to its highest point, pauses at the apex, and reverses direction. The winter solstice is the opposite , the longest night, the deepest dark, the turning point back toward light. These are not just astronomical events. They are metaphors for how people are.

Some people live at their summer solstice , bright, outward, abundant, running at full charge. Others live at their winter solstice , deep, quiet, interior, their richest life unfolding in stillness. Most people fall somewhere between these poles, and somewhere between inward and outward, on a cycle that turns through four recognizable seasons of the self.

The Solstice Cycle Assessment maps you onto that cycle. It measures two things: how bright your characteristic energy burns, and which direction it tends to flow. From those two coordinates, it places you in one of four seasons. Every position is valid. There is no bad season. The test is built on a specific body of research , on how people experience activation, on how motivation works in the brain, on why personality instruments that force you into boxes lose real information. This article explains what the test measures, why it was designed the way it was, what each result means, and what the research actually says about each piece of the framework.

---

## What the test measures

Two dimensions. Each scored from 0 to 100. Neither is better than the other. Together they place you somewhere on a wheel.

**Solar Height** is your characteristic energy level , how bright you burn at baseline. This is not your mood at this moment. It is your dispositional activation: when nothing is demanding anything of you, are you humming or settled? Do you wake at full charge, or do you come online slowly? The concept comes from the most validated model of how people experience affect , Russell's (1980) circumplex, a two-dimensional circle on which all affective states can be located. One of its two axes is arousal, the dimension from sleepiness to high activation. Russell demonstrated this structure using four independent scaling methods in the same paper , circular ordering, multidimensional scaling, unidimensional scaling on pleasure and arousal dimensions, and analysis of 343 people's self-reports , and all four methods converged. The arousal axis is not a metaphor. It is a measurable, replicable dimension of human experience.

The SCA labels the high end *Bright* and the low end *Dim*, but these are evocative names for high and low activation. Bright is energized, alert, internally active. Dim is calm, quiet, still. Dim is not depression. It is depth and restoration. The test measures whether you tend toward one or the other, and to what degree.

**Tidal Direction** is where your energy tends to flow , outward toward the world and other people, or inward toward your interior and your own reflection. This axis comes from Carver and White's (1994) work on the brain's motivational systems. They developed a self-report instrument measuring two systems that Gray (1981) had identified: the Behavioral Activation System, which responds to reward and drives approach behavior, and the Behavioral Inhibition System, which responds to punishment and drives withdrawal and vigilance. The critical finding for the SCA is that these two systems are independent , Carver and White state explicitly that they are "presumed to be orthogonal" (p. 320), and their data, from four studies including actual laboratory experiments where people anticipated real reward or punishment, confirmed that people can be high in both, low in both, or any combination. Approach and withdrawal are not opposite ends of one dimension. They are two separate dimensions, and a person's characteristic balance between them is what the SCA measures on a single continuous scale.

The SCA labels the high end *Outward* , energy flowing toward engagement, action, and social connection. The low end is *Inward* , energy flowing toward reflection, interiority, and conservation. Neither is better.

Because Russell's circumplex and Carver and White's motivational systems are independently validated structures, combining them into a two-axis framework is not arbitrary. The SCA uses one axis from the affect literature and one from the motivational literature, places them orthogonally, and maps a person's position on the resulting plane. The four quadrants of that plane are the four seasons. The wheel is the natural geometry of the circumplex. The seasons are the interpretive layer.

## The four seasons

Each season is a specific combination of an energy level and a direction. Each comes with a full profile describing what that combination tends to look like in real life. The profiles are archetypes , patterns that emerge from the axis geometry, not empirical clusters discovered by fitting a model to data. But the literature independently supports that a small number of personality types in the 3 to 5 range is replicable across large datasets. Gerlach, Farb, Revelle, and Amaral (2018) found four types across 1.5 million respondents using a density-based significance method that filters out spurious cluster solutions. Kerber, Roth, and Herzberg (2021) reviewed 19 prior studies and ran three different clustering algorithms on a dataset of 22,820 people, finding five robust types. The SCA's four types are theory-derived, not data-clustered, but the empirical literature independently confirms that four sits in the viable range. The test reports continuous scores *and* a type label , the type is the interpretation, not the measurement.

### ☀️ Summer , The Zenith

Bright and Outward. High energy turned fully toward the world.

**The archetype.** Summer is the peak of the cycle. These are people whose energy burns high and flows outward. When they enter a room, the temperature tends to rise , not through force, but through sheer generative presence. Things bloom around them because they bring light and momentum. They are most themselves in motion: making, connecting, starting, giving.

The gift is abundance. The shadow is the same: a sun that never sets burns out. Summer people tend to equate stillness with failure or low mood, and they may underestimate how much restoration they actually need. They recover energy through engagement rather than withdrawal, which works until it doesn't.

**Motivations.** To express fully , to bring what is inside into the world without dampening it. To generate and start things. To live in the center of life rather than at its edges. To feel and be felt, vividly.

**How they behave.** High, visible energy. They are often the one initiating conversations, plans, and activities. They think out loud and decide in motion. They expand social situations , more people, more activity, more yes. Their mood is readable, and it tends to influence the room. They recover energy through engagement.

**Strengths.** Magnetism and warmth. People feel lit up around them. Generative drive , they start and ship. Courage of expression and low fear of visibility. An ability to make others feel included and energized. Resilience through action , they move through difficulty by doing.

**Blind spots.** Equating stillness with failure or low mood. Overcommitting , saying yes past their real capacity. Underestimating quieter people's depth, and their own need for it. Believing that intensity equals commitment or love. Burning out and reading the exhaustion as a personal failure rather than a signal to rest.

**Growth.** Build deliberate recovery into the rhythm. Practice the power of one well-chosen no. Learn that withdrawal is a skill, not a deficit. Let others hold the spotlight , practice being warmed rather than always warming.

**Relationships.** Summer people bring warmth, generosity, and momentum to their relationships. They may overwhelm more inward partners and misread slowness as disinterest. They need people who can meet their energy and remind them to rest. The growth edge is learning to love that can hold their quiet, not just their shine.

**Work.** They thrive in dynamic, visible, generative roles , launches, starts, rallying teams, pitching, leading from the front. Risk: starting more than they finish, and mistaking busyness for output. Ideal environments offer high autonomy and energy, with recovery explicitly built in.

**Decision-making.** Fast, intuitive, often in dialogue with others. They decide by doing , action clarifies. Risk: deciding from peak emotion. Major calls benefit from a 24-hour pause. Strength: they do not get stuck in analysis.

**Under stress.** The signature is acceleration , more activity, more volume, more taking-on. They may radiate stress outward, affecting those near them. Recovery requires what is hardest for them: stopping, dimming, going inward. Watch for irritability and depletion disguised as "just busy."

**Communication.** Direct, expressive, warm, often high-volume. They inspire and mobilize well. Growth: make space for silence. Not every silence needs filling. Listen for the people who speak after they stop.

**The complementary season.** Winter. Summer's wholeness lives in the part of the cycle they tend to skip , Winter's stillness, depth, and restoration. The sun that rests returns brighter. The deepest growth is permission to dim without fear.

### 🍂 Autumn , The Turning

Bright and Inward. High energy that has turned inward , intensity gathering itself for release.

**The archetype.** Autumn is the harvest self. These people carry real intensity, but it moves inward. They are the golden hour: warm and lit, yet drawing the sap down toward the root. They do not lack energy. They are directing it , gathering what matters, distilling it, deciding what to keep and what to let fall.

Where Summer burns outward, Autumn consolidates. They feel deeply and have learned to hold it, refine it, and release what no longer serves. The gift is intensity with judgment. The shadow is that withdrawal can masquerade as wisdom , sometimes it is avoidance. They may be misread as distant when they are in fact carrying a great deal.

Why is Autumn placed at Bright rather than Dim? Astronomically, autumn is decreasing light, and one could argue it belongs at the low-activation end. But energetically, autumn is still abundant , the harvest is full, the colors are at peak intensity, the warmth has not yet left. The SCA places seasons by their current energy state, not their trajectory. Autumn's trajectory , the turning toward winter , is captured by the angle gradation, not the axis. This is a design choice grounded in the activation construct.

**Motivations.** To distill meaning from experience , to keep only what is true. To honor the full cycle, including release. To hold intensity without being run by it. To integrate, not just accumulate.

**How they behave.** Warm but selective , they go deep with a few. They process before they share; their output is considered. They let go of roles, identities, and relationships more readily than most. They are comfortable with endings and transitions. Their intensity is real, but private , others may underestimate it.

**Strengths.** Discernment , they know what to keep and what to release. Depth without coldness, warmth without diffusion. Comfort with change, loss, and transition. Mature self-awareness , they have integrated a lot. The ability to hold paradox: bright and inward at once.

**Blind spots.** Withdrawal can masquerade as wisdom. Releasing too much , cutting things prematurely. Being misread as fine or distant when carrying a lot. Nostalgia tipping into stuckness. Withholding warmth as self-protection.

**Growth.** Distinguish discernment from avoidance , "am I letting go, or am I leaving?" Share the harvest , what has been gathered is meant to feed others. Let people in on the intensity rather than refining it alone. Resist the romance of melancholy.

**Relationships.** They offer rare depth, loyalty, and the courage to face hard truths together. Partners may feel held but also kept at a refined distance. They thrive with people who can meet their interiority without flinching. Growth: let yourself be known, not just understood.

**Work.** Excellent at synthesis, editing, strategy, and finishing. They shine in roles requiring judgment and integration. Risk: over-refining , shipping late because it is "not ready." Ideal environments offer autonomous depth-work with meaningful collaboration.

**Decision-making.** Deliberate, values-driven, comfortable with trade-offs. They decide well when they trust their discernment. Risk: analysis loops , deciding by elimination rather than commitment. Strength: they rarely make reckless calls.

**Under stress.** The signature is pulling inward and refining harder , overthinking, withdrawal. They may become quietly heavy. Recovery requires movement, release, and trusted conversation , getting it out. Watch for isolation disguised as "processing."

**Communication.** Precise, considered, sometimes sparing. They say less and mean more. Growth: do not make people guess , share the warmth being refined. Their words land. Use that power generously.

**The complementary season.** Spring. Autumn's wholeness lives in re-engagement , Spring's tender, outward, hopeful new growth. Having learned to let go, the next mastery is beginning again, and trusting it.

### ❄️ Winter , The Deep

Dim and Inward. Richest in stillness and interiority. Power that is quiet, rooted, and renewing.

**The archetype.** Winter is the longest night. These people find their richest territory in interior life , depth, clarity, and restoration emerge in stillness, not because they lack energy but because their energy is concentrated rather than dispersed. They are the part of the cycle where the seed does its essential work in the dark.

Winter is reframed, always, as depth and renewal. Never deficit. Winter's power is quiet and rooted: they see clearly because they are not dazzled by surface light. They endure because they have learned to rest. The world tends to undervalue stillness. Winter's gift is proving how generative it actually is.

**Motivations.** To understand deeply , truth over performance. To protect and tend what is essential. To restore, root, and renew. To be real, not reactive.

**How they behave.** Low, steady output , long horizon. They observe more than they emit and remember what others miss. They are comfortable alone and recharge in solitude and slowness. Their commitments are few but deep, and loyal once given. Their presence is calming and clarifying for others.

**Strengths.** Depth of insight and clear seeing. Steadiness, endurance, patience. Independence and self-sufficiency. A calming, grounding presence. The ability to renew themselves through difficulty.

**Blind spots.** Staying in the cold too long , withdrawal tipping into isolation. Under-asserting , their depth goes unseen and unmet. Mistaking slowness for incapability, theirs or others'. Holding too tightly to the interior and not emerging. Reading high-energy people as superficial.

**Growth.** Practice emergent action before feeling "ready." Let yourself be seen and supported , receiving is a skill. Share your insights , the world needs what you see. Distinguish rest from hiding.

**Relationships.** Winter people offer rare loyalty, depth, and steadiness. They need people who respect their slowness and solitude. Risk: partners feel shut out , the interior must be translated. Growth: initiate connection, not just respond to it.

**Work.** Excellent at deep, focused, long-horizon work. They shine in analysis, research, craft, and anything requiring sustained attention. Risk: invisibility , their best work may go uncredited. Ideal environments offer protected focus time, low meetings, and a culture that honors depth.

**Decision-making.** Slow, thorough, high-quality. They decide once and well, with low regret. Risk: over-deliberation , missing time-sensitive windows. Strength: their calls tend to hold up over time.

**Under stress.** The signature is withdrawing further, going quieter, carrying it alone. They may seem fine while struggling. Recovery requires gentle re-engagement and trusted warmth , not forced socializing. Watch for the long night becoming permanent. Set a thaw date.

**Communication.** Considered, economical, often written better than spoken. When they speak, it matters. Growth: do not wait until it is perfect. Say the half-formed thing. Their listening is a gift , name it for others.

**The complementary season.** Summer. Winter's wholeness lives in emergence , Summer's full, outward, radiant expression. The seed's whole purpose is the bloom. The deepest growth is daring to take up space.

---

### 🌱 Spring , The Greening

Dim and Outward. Tender, persistent new growth moving outward into the world. Adaptive, hopeful, renewing.

**The archetype.** Spring moves outward but softly , not with Summer's blaze, but with the green persistence of the first shoot through cold ground. Their energy is modest in wattage but extraordinary in resilience. They adapt, renew, and begin again. They are proof that tender things are strong.

Spring is the courage of softness. These people engage the world without needing to dominate it. They are the friend who resurfaces after every winter, the one who tries again. The gift is renewal , for themselves and everyone they touch.

Like Autumn, Spring's placement follows the energy-state logic: spring's activation is still low , the shoots are tender, the warmth not yet arrived , even though the trajectory is rising toward summer. The trajectory shows up in the gradation.

**Motivations.** To grow, adapt, and renew , themselves and their context. To stay open despite difficulty. To begin, and begin again. To move toward the world gently but persistently.

**How they behave.** Engaged but not loud , present without dominating. They shift with conditions and bounce back easily. They start things , relationships, projects, habits , with genuine hope. They notice small signs of life that others miss.

**Strengths.** Resilience and renewal , they recover and restart. Adaptability and openness. Hope that is earned, not naive. Gentleness with real persistence. They draw others out , their openness invites others'.

**Blind spots.** Over-extending tenderness , staying too long in cold situations. Mistaking adaptability for not having needs. Beginning more than they can sustain. Underestimating their own wattage. Avoiding necessary hardness or conflict.

**Growth.** Discern where to grow , not every crack is soil. Claim your needs , adaptability without boundaries burns out. Let some springs become summers , commit and intensify. Practice staying when it is hard, not just starting when it is fresh.

**Relationships.** Spring people offer warmth, openness, and the gift of second chances. They see and nurture others' growth. Risk: pouring into people who do not reciprocate. Growth: be renewed *by* their relationships, not just *for* them.

**Work.** They thrive in evolving, mission-driven, people-oriented environments. They shine in roles requiring empathy, adaptation, and renewal , teaching, coaching, care, growth. Risk: under-claiming credit and burning out from giving. Ideal environments value tenderness and tenacity equally.

**Decision-making.** Collaborative, values-led, comfortable revising. They decide adaptively and rarely get locked in. Risk: changing course too often and under-committing. Strength: they rarely make rigid, irreversible errors.

**Under stress.** The signature is trying harder, giving more, keeping growing through it. They may neglect their own roots while tending others'. Recovery requires returning to their own soil , rest, slowness, fewer outputs. Watch for chronic over-giving read as "just being helpful."

**Communication.** Warm, encouraging, inclusive, often modest. They make people feel safe to grow. Growth: hold their ground when it matters , soft does not mean small. Their encouragement is catalytic. They should use it on themselves too.

**The complementary season.** Autumn. Spring's wholeness lives in discernment , Autumn's capacity to keep what matters and release what does not. Having mastered beginning, the next mastery is choosing what to let end.

## How the questions were designed

The SCA is offered in two lengths from one item bank. The standard form, **SCA-32**, has 32 questions, 8 for each of four facets, two facets under each axis. This is not an arbitrary number. The NEO-PI-R, the most widely used validated personality instrument, uses exactly 8 items per facet. The Big Five Aspect Scales developed by DeYoung, Quilty, and Peterson (2007) use 10. Gosling, Rentfrow, and Swann (2003) showed in developing the Ten-Item Personality Inventory that 2 items per dimension is the operational floor — adequate for screening when time is extremely limited, but "somewhat inferior to standard multi-item instruments" (p. 504). SCA-32's 8 items per facet sits at the validated-instrument norm. Under the Spearman-Brown prophecy formula, 8 items per facet gives expected reliability in the .85 to .92 range.

The short form, **SCA-16**, is a curated subset of the 32 — the same items, four per facet rather than eight — not a parallel form. This follows the standard short-from-long-form method (NEO-FFI is drawn from NEO-PI-R; HEXACO-60 from HEXACO-200; BFI-2-S from BFI-2): the subset is the same instrument administered shorter, inherits the long form's content-validity argument, and allows a respondent to upgrade to the full result by answering the remaining items. The tradeoff is reliability. Four items per facet lands in the .60 to .70 alpha band by Spearman-Brown — the "marginal" band — which is precisely why the long form uses eight. Gosling et al. (2003) frame ultra-short scales as screening-tier with "diminished psychometric properties." SCA-16 is therefore presented as a screening-tier quick read: it places a respondent in a season or the Threshold reliably, but its facet-level signals (the facet signature and the facet-tension modifier) are noisier and are surfaced as provisional. The scoring model and result taxonomy are identical across both lengths; only the number of items feeding each facet mean changes.

### The four facets

DeYoung, Quilty, and Peterson (2007) analyzed 75 facet scales from the NEO-PI-R and the AB5C-IPIP and found that within every Big Five personality domain, the facets naturally organize into exactly two factors , what they call *aspects*. For Extraversion, the two aspects are Enthusiasm (sociability, positive emotion, friendliness) and Assertiveness (agency, leadership, drive toward impact). They are correlated at about r = .53 , related but separable. And DeYoung et al. demonstrated that these two aspects correspond to two distinct genetic factors that Jang et al. (2002) had found underlying the shared variance among Extraversion facets. In other words, the two-aspect structure shows up at the genetic level. This is not a loose conceptual distinction.

The SCA borrows this architecture directly for Axis B. Facet B1, Social Approach, maps to DeYoung's Enthusiasm aspect , it measures whether you move toward people or toward solitude. Facet B2, Agency, maps to DeYoung's Assertiveness aspect , it measures whether you initiate and lead or take a more receptive, responsive posture. The eight items in each facet target distinct content areas rather than paraphrasing one idea: for Social Approach, the items cover distress sharing, new-people approach, group positioning, close-relationship initiation, daily social density, default orientation, energy source, and connection style. For Agency, they cover decision tempo, leadership, impact drive, persuasion, control orientation, initiative, self-direction, and competitive drive.

For Axis A, the two-facet structure follows the same logic but is the framework's own application of DeYoung's pattern to the activation domain. Facet A1, Baseline Arousal, draws on the energetic arousal construct , the awake-tired dimension of felt activation that Schimmack and Reisenzein (2002) demonstrated is a distinct activation type, not reducible to a mixture of valence and general activation. The A1 items cover waking state, default attention, inner phenomenology, default readiness, transition speed, somatic activation, evening energy, and comparative self-assessment. Facet A2, Stimulation Appetite, draws on the sensation-seeking construct , the preference for varied, novel, and intense stimulation that Zuckerman (2007) validated across hundreds of studies with the Sensation Seeking Scale. The A2 items cover sensory intensity, environmental density, pace, information volume, scheduling density, boredom susceptibility, variety, and comparative frame.

Unlike Axis B, which maps both facets onto a single validated domain (DeYoung's two-aspect Extraversion), Axis A composites two constructs from different research traditions — energetic arousal and sensation seeking — that have not been jointly validated as aspects of a single higher-order activation factor. The compositing is a design choice. The facet-level reporting recovers the lost texture: a high-Bright result can still distinguish whether it is driven by high baseline arousal, high stimulation appetite, or both.

### The format

Each question presents a short scenario stem and two anchor statements , one at each end of a seven-point scale. For example: "When you wake, you're..." / "Already at full charge" versus "Still coming online, quietly." You tap or drag to the position that feels like you. The midpoint means "equally both" or "it depends."

The choice of a seven-point bipolar format was not arbitrary. Sun, Schmidt, and Henry (2025) ran Monte Carlo simulations to determine the optimal number of response categories. Under the realistic assumption that measurement error increases as you add more categories , because people cannot reliably tell the difference between 63 and 64 on a 100-point slider , the optimum falls between 5 and 10 categories, with 7 optimal for the most likely dependency structure. More importantly, their title states the finding: converting a validated Likert-type item to a continuous visual analog scale without re-validating the instrument produces a drastic drop in reliability.

The SCA deliberately avoids forced-choice formats , the kind where you must pick A or B with no middle ground. McCrae and Costa (1989) evaluated the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator against the Five-Factor Model and found that none of the MBTI's indices showed true bimodality , the scores were continuous, not split into two groups. When they analyzed the continuous scores, those scores showed stronger relations with external criteria than the forced-choice type classifications did. The authors concluded that "information is lost when the MBTI indices are dichotomized" (p. 29). Schulte, Holling, and Bürkner (2020) later demonstrated that forced-choice questionnaires produce scores that cannot be compared across people in the usual way, even when measuring as many as 30 traits at once.

The bipolar format also handles a subtle statistical problem. In traditional agree-disagree scales, some people tend to agree with statements regardless of content , this is called acquiescence bias, and it is normally controlled by including reverse-keyed items (statements phrased so that agreement means the opposite of the construct). In a bipolar format, you are choosing between two statements rather than agreeing or disagreeing with one, which inherently counters acquiescence. The SCA additionally randomizes which pole appears on the left versus the right at render time, countering position bias , the tendency to default to one side of the screen.

## How scoring works

The scoring is hierarchical. Your eight responses within each facet are averaged and scaled to a 0-to-100 range. Your two facet scores on each axis are then averaged to produce your axis score , again, 0 to 100. For Axis A (Solar Height), 0 is Dim and 100 is Bright. For Axis B (Tidal Direction), 0 is Inward and 100 is Outward.

Your position is plotted on a two-dimensional plane with center at (50, 50). The four quadrants determine your season. If your Solar Height is above 50 and your Tidal Direction is above 50, you are Summer , Bright and Outward. Above 50 in Brightness and below 50 in Direction puts you in Autumn , Bright and Inward. Below 50 in both is Winter , Dim and Inward. Below 50 in Brightness and above 50 in Direction is Spring , Dim and Outward.

The four quadrants treat the two axes as orthogonal. In practice, Solar Height and Tidal Direction are correlated — both load on Big Five Extraversion, Solar Height through Extraversion's energy and enthusiasm facets, Tidal Direction through Extraversion's sociability facet. The quadrant boundaries are approximate. The hybrid dimensional/prototypical reporting pattern, where the type is a label applied to continuous scores rather than a discrete cluster assignment, accommodates this. A person whose position is near a quadrant boundary receives gradation and leaning rather than a hard type switch.

But the test gives you more than a quadrant label. Your exact position within your quadrant determines your gradation: early, mid, or late in the season. The phase angle , computed as atan2 of your two axis scores relative to the center , maps you to a specific angular position on the wheel. Each 90-degree quadrant is divided into three 30-degree bands: early (the first 30 degrees from the entry edge), mid, and late. The entry edge is the boundary with the previous season in the yearly cycle (Spring → Summer → Autumn → Winter → Spring), so "Early" means just arrived from the previous season and "Late" means about to cross into the next: "Late Autumn" means you are approaching Winter; "Early Summer" means the energy is still building toward peak. (An earlier version of this convention was inverted; v0.18 corrected it.)

Your distance from the center , called prototypicality , tells you how clearly you embody your season. Far from center means you are strongly that season. Close to center means you are between types. If your prototypicality falls below a threshold of 0.28 , which corresponds to being within about 14 points of the center on both axes (or within about 20 points on one axis while the other sits near center) , the test reports the Threshold result. This is the equinox position on the wheel: a point of balance between seasons, where neither the energy pole nor the direction pole dominates. The Axis-A extremes , the most Bright and most Dim , are the literal solstice positions on the wheel, where the cycle reaches a peak and reverses direction. The June solstice is summer in the Northern Hemisphere and winter in the Southern; the SCA uses hemisphere-neutral seasons.

There is one more case the midpoint metaphor has to handle honestly. Sometimes a person lands near the center not because both axes are moderate, but because the two facets within an axis pull hard in opposite directions and cancel at the axis level — high baseline arousal paired with low stimulation appetite, for instance, averaging to a middling activation score. A shallow reading would call that "balanced." It is not. When an axis shows this cancellation (its two facets on opposite sides of 50 and both strongly extreme), the SCA fires a **facet-tension modifier** on the Threshold result. The result is still the Threshold — the wheel's near-center boundary — but its narrative is rewritten from the equinox reading ("you are balanced") to the tension reading ("you are near the center because two strong facets cancel, not because you are moderate"), led by the facet signature. This keeps the type count at four. The four seasonal quadrants are types in the sense the person-centered literature uses the word: clusters recoverable by density or mixture methods, the structure Gerlach et al. (2018) found with Gaussian mixture models and Kerber et al. (2021) found with three clustering algorithms. The near-center region is, by construction, the sparsest part of any circumplex; no density-based method would return it as a cluster, so it is a boundary condition, not a type. Facet cancellation is a within-person score pattern, not a between-person cluster, so it is a modifier, not a type either. Treating the midpoint and the modifier as types would push this test to five or six results and contradict the very evidence (three-to-five cluster solutions, replicable across large samples) the framework cites for keeping to four.

The facet signature is reported alongside the type , which facet drove which axis score. A high-Bright result might be driven mostly by Stimulation Appetite (you seek intensity) rather than Baseline Arousal (your resting energy is moderate). A high-Outward result might be driven mostly by Agency (you initiate and lead) rather than Social Approach (your people-orientation is balanced). This level of reporting , distinguishing which sub-component of a dimension is doing the work , is what separates a serious instrument from a single-number quiz. It is the same logic the NEO-PI-R uses when it reports that your high Extraversion is driven more by Assertiveness than by Warmth.

The wheel is the results display, not the input. You answer questions one at a time on a simple bipolar scale. The wheel appears at the end, when your position is plotted. This avoids anchoring effects , seeing the wheel during the test could tempt people to target a specific quadrant. The reveal at the end is more honest.

## The research in one place

The SCA draws on a specific body of work. Each source is listed below with what it contributes.

Russell (1980) demonstrated that affective states are organized as a circle on two bipolar dimensions , arousal and pleasure-displeasure , using four independent scaling methods that all converged. This is the structural geometry of the SCA. Yik, Russell, and Steiger (2011) refined this into a validated 12-point circumplex measurement model.

Carver and White (1994) developed the BIS/BAS scales and demonstrated that the behavioral activation and behavioral inhibition systems are orthogonal , approach and withdrawal are independent dimensions, not opposite poles of one. The BAS has three subscales (Drive, Fun Seeking, Reward Responsiveness). This grounds Axis B and informs the facet decomposition.

DeYoung, Quilty, and Peterson (2007) found that each Big Five domain splits into two aspects, demonstrated this across 75 facet scales from two inventories, and linked the aspects to distinct genetic factors. The Enthusiasm/Assertiveness split grounds the SCA's B1/B2 facet structure and the two-facet-per-axis architecture.

Schimmack and Reisenzein (2002) demonstrated that energetic arousal is a distinct activation type , when you remove the shared variance with valence, the correlation between the residuals of energetic arousal and tense arousal is not significantly different from zero. This grounds the A1 facet.

Zuckerman (2007) reviewed the validation record of the Sensation Seeking Scale, which has been used across hundreds of studies with replicated four-subscale structure and discriminant validity. This grounds the A2 facet.

Gerlach, Farb, Revelle, and Amaral (2018) applied density-based significance testing to Gaussian mixture models on four datasets totaling 1.5 million respondents, finding four types that survive a null-model verification procedure. Kerber, Roth, and Herzberg (2021) reviewed 19 prior studies and applied three clustering algorithms to a German dataset of 22,820 people, finding five replicable types. Together they demonstrate that a small number of personality types (3 to 5) is empirically defensible.

Sun, Schmidt, and Henry (2025) demonstrated through Monte Carlo simulation that under realistic error-dependency assumptions, continuous visual analog scales underperform discrete Likert scales, with an optimum at 5 to 10 categories. McCrae and Costa (1989) showed that the MBTI does not measure true dichotomies , no indices showed bimodality , and that continuous scores outperform forced-choice classifications. Schulte, Holling, and Bürkner (2020) demonstrated that forced-choice formats produce ipsative scores even with 30 traits. These three papers ground the response-format decisions.

Gosling, Rentfrow, and Swann (2003) developed the Ten-Item Personality Inventory and demonstrated that 2 items per dimension is the operational floor , adequate for screening, but with "diminished psychometric properties." This grounds the 8-items-per-facet choice as matching the validated-instrument standard. Chang (1994) compared 4-point and 6-point scales and showed that no universal optimum exists , the appropriate number depends on the measurement setting, and scale-length choices must be contextualized.

---

## The test in practice

The SCA takes about 8 to 10 minutes. All 32 questions are presented one at a time. Results are immediate: your season, your phase within that season, your prototypicality, and your facet signature. You also receive a narrative profile describing your result type in detail.

The test is designed for reflective self-discovery. It is not a clinical instrument. It cannot diagnose anything. It should not be used for hiring decisions, relationship matching, or any high-stakes real-world application. A short disclaimer stating this accompanies every result.

The SCA has not been validated through factor analysis of its items, test-retest reliability assessment, or convergent and discriminant validity studies against established personality measures. Its scores have no population norms. These are the necessary next steps, and they require pilot data. The framework is designed to be validation-ready , items respect construct boundaries, facets are grounded in the literature, scoring is hierarchical , but that readiness is not itself validation.

A companion simulation script (`sca-simulate.ts`) runs synthetic respondents through the scoring engine to verify that normalization, facet and axis scoring, distance, angle, gradation, and the tension/threshold classification behave as specified, and that boundary and corner cases resolve. It is a self-consistency and edge-case harness, not validation: its "expected" results are tautological, because the same designer-set centers generate the synthetic respondents and define the expectations. Synthetic respondents have no psychology. The simulation does not establish reliability, construct validity, or population norms, and is not cited as evidence toward them.
