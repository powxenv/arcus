# SSA Result Profiles

> Companion to `03-spectrum-of-self-assessment.md`.
> Full-depth profiles for each SSA result, using the same 14-section schema as Tests 1 and 2 so all four tests are directly comparable.
> These are the deterministic fallback narratives. The Gemini layer enriches the Archetype and Reflection sections from the player's answer pattern. It never overrides the structured sections.
> Honesty note: profiles describe tendencies associated with a combination of two measured dimension, identity clarity and self-alignment. They are affirming, non-clinical, and avoid deficit framing. Every diffuse result is described as openness and flexibility, never as emptiness or lack of identity. v0.23: the result gains a commitment component (Marcia) and, when the opt-in informant path is used, a real SOKA visibility gap (Vazire).

---

## Canonical schema (every type)

1. **Essence.** One-line core plus the two axis scores.
2. **The Archetype.** Two short narrative paragraphs.
3. **Core Motivations.** What drives this mode.
4. **Behavioral Patterns.** Observable tendencies.
5. **Strengths.**
6. **Blind Spots.** Honest, non-shaming.
7. **Growth Edges.** Specific development.
8. **In Relationships.** What they offer, need, and risk.
9. **Work Style.** Ideal conditions, contributions, pitfalls.
10. **Decision-Making.** Tendencies and traps.
11. **Under Stress.** Stress signature and recovery.
12. **Communication.** Style and growth.
13. **The Complementary Mode.** The opposite quadrant they can learn from.
14. **Your Reflection.** The poetic closing line about self-knowledge. AI-personalized in-game.

## 🔦 The Beacon  (Clear + Self-Aligned)

**Essence.** You know yourself, and you show yourself. There is little gap between who you are and what the world sees.

### The Archetype

You have a clear sense of who you are — your values, your strengths, your edges — and you express that self openly. There is no performance, no mask, no gap between your inner experience and your outer presentation. What people see is what they get, and what they get is genuine.

This is the mode of self-assurance without self-aggrandizement. You do not need to perform who you are because you have done the work of knowing yourself, and you are comfortable enough to let yourself be known. Your presence is steady, grounded, and honest. People trust you because you are consistent — not rigid, but reliably yourself across contexts. You are the person others turn to for an honest opinion, because your opinion will be genuinely yours.

### Core Motivations
- To be fully known and to fully know yourself.
- To live with integrity — alignment between values and actions.
- To understand yourself deeply and honestly.
- To create authentic connections based on mutual transparency.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You are consistent across contexts: work, home, with friends — the same person shows up.
- You voice your opinions even when they are not popular.
- You make decisions that align with your values rather than what is expected.
- You reflect on yourself regularly but without rumination or self-criticism.
- People feel they know the real you.

### Strengths
- Integrity and self-possession. Your actions match your values.
- Clarity of purpose. You know what matters to you and why.
- Authentic relationships built on trust and openness.
- Resilience under pressure — your sense of self does not crumble when challenged.
- The ability to give honest feedback without being harsh.

### Blind Spots
- Being so clear about who you are that you stop evolving.
- Underestimating how hard it is for others to know themselves as clearly.
- Impatience with people who are still figuring themselves out.
- Mistaking clarity for completeness — there may be aspects of yourself you haven't examined.
- A tendency to assume others want the same level of transparency you offer.

### Growth Edges
- Leave room for surprise. You are still becoming, not just being.
- Practice sitting with not-knowing. Some questions about yourself don't have answers yet.
- Let others show themselves at their own pace. Your openness invites trust, but it can also overwhelm.

### In Relationships
- You offer honesty, stability, and genuine presence. People feel safe being real with you.
- You may frustrate partners who are less self-aware or who prefer more privacy.
- You need people who respect your clarity without needing the same level of it for themselves.
- Growth: not every moment needs full transparency. Some things are yours to hold privately, and that is not inauthentic.

### Work Style
- Thrive in roles requiring integrity, self-direction, and honest communication: leadership, coaching, ethics, counseling, creative direction.
- You shine at giving candid feedback and making value-aligned decisions.
- Risk: being perceived as rigid or judgmental. Your clarity can read as certainty, and certainty can read as close-mindedness.
- Ideal: environments that reward authenticity and give you autonomy to act on your values.

### Decision-Making
- Value-led, considered, and aligned with a clear sense of purpose.
- You decide by checking what fits your core values.
- Risk: dismissing options that conflict with your self-concept prematurely.
- Strength: your decisions are consistent and rarely regretted.

### Under Stress
- Signature: double down on what you know to be true. Your clarity becomes a shield.
- You may become less receptive to feedback when under pressure.
- Recovery: return to your core values. They are your anchor.
- Watch: clarity turning into rigidity. You can be right and still miss something.

### Communication
- Direct, honest, consistent. What you say matches what you feel.
- Growth: soften the edges. Your clarity can land as certainty, and certainty can close conversations.
- Your honesty is a gift. Pair it with curiosity, and it becomes influence.

### The Complementary Mode: 💠 The Aurora
Your wholeness lives in what you do not yet know. The Aurora's flexibility and openness — the willingness to be shaped by context rather than always shaping it — is the adaptability your clarity needs. Certainty without fluidity calcifies. Fluidity is what lets your identity grow beyond the shape it currently holds.

### Your Reflection
"You are the steady light that knows itself and shines without distortion. Your gift is clarity, and your growth is learning what lies beyond the edges of what you already know."

## 🔮 The Prism  (Clear + Other-Oriented)

**Essence.** You know yourself, but you refract differently depending on who you're with. Your inner clarity stays steady while your presentation shifts.

### The Archetype

You have a clear sense of who you are, but you do not show that self to everyone the same way. Like a prism, you take a steady inner light and refract it differently depending on the angle and the context. You know your values, your strengths, your identity — but you adjust your presentation to fit the social situation.

This is not inauthenticity. It is social intelligence. You understand that different contexts call for different versions of yourself, and you are skilled at matching the moment without losing your core. Where The Beacon shines the same way always, you adapt — not because you are false, but because you are attuned to the people and expectations around you. Your clarity gives you a home base; your adaptability lets you navigate the world effectively.

### Core Motivations
- To be true to yourself while also fitting into the social world.
- To understand people and adjust to them without losing your core.
- To be effective in diverse social contexts.
- To protect your inner self from being fully exposed to everyone.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You are consistent in your values but flexible in your presentation.
- You read social situations carefully and adapt accordingly.
- Different groups see different sides of you, but they are all genuinely you.
- You switch easily between professional, personal, and social selves.
- People may not realize how much you adjust — your adaptations are smooth and practiced.

### Strengths
- Social intelligence and adaptability. You handle diverse contexts well.
- Role flexibility. You can lead, follow, contribute across different groups.
- Self-awareness without over-exposure. You know yourself and choose what to share.
- The ability to build rapport across different types of people.
- Protection of your private self while remaining genuine in each context.

### Blind Spots
- People may feel they don't know the real you.
- The effort of managing multiple presentations can be draining.
- Losing touch with which version is your core versus which is adaptive.
- Being perceived as inauthentic by people who value raw consistency.
- Difficulty saying no or showing disapproval when it would disrupt the social harmony.

### Growth Edges
- Let some people see more of you. Not every context requires adaptation.
- Check in with your core regularly: are you choosing this adaptation, or is it automatic?
- Practice being the same person across two situations that call for different versions of you.

### In Relationships
- You offer insight, adaptability, and a deep understanding of social dynamics.
- Partners may feel you are hard to pin down or that they see only part of you.
- You need people who appreciate your range and don't demand a single performance.
- Growth: let the people closest to you see the full beam, not just the refracted light.

### Work Style
- Thrive in roles requiring social awareness, diplomacy, and stakeholder management: consulting, management, sales, public relations, teaching.
- You shine at reading a room and meeting people where they are.
- Risk: being seen as a chameleon, someone whose authentic views are hard to discern.
- Ideal: environments with diverse stakeholders and complex social landscapes.

### Decision-Making
- Context-sensitive, socially aware, ethically grounded.
- You consider both your values and the social impact of your choice.
- Risk: deciding based on what others expect rather than what you truly want.
- Strength: your decisions tend to be socially sustainable and well-received.

### Under Stress
- Signature: adapt harder. Read the room more, adjust more, try to find the right presentation.
- You may lose touch with your core preferences when stressed.
- Recovery: remove social pressure. Ask yourself what you want when no one is watching.
- Watch: adaptation turning into self-loss. You can fit in so well you forget what you actually need.

### Communication
- Skilled, attuned, context-sensitive. You say the right thing for the situation.
- Growth: practice saying what you think, not just what fits.
- Your adaptability is a skill. Make sure it serves you, not just the group.

### The Complementary Mode: 🔥 The Ember
Your wholeness lives in the part of you that shows without adaptation. The Ember's raw genuineness — the willingness to be seen without polish — is the depth your flexibility needs. Adaptation without authenticity becomes performance. Warmth without refraction is the part of you that makes connection real.

### Your Reflection
"You are the steady light that knows its own colors and chooses how to shine in each room. Your gift is adaptability, and your growth is knowing when to let the full beam through."

## 🔥 The Ember  (Diffuse + Self-Aligned)

**Essence.** You don't have a fully formed sense of yourself, but what you do show is genuine. The light is soft, but it is real.

### The Archetype

You are still figuring yourself out — and you are honest about it. Your sense of identity is not fully crystallized, and that uncertainty is part of who you are right now. But even in that openness, you do not pretend. What you show to the world is what you genuinely feel and believe in this moment, even if that may change tomorrow.

There is an honesty in not knowing. The Ember burns softly and authentically. You do not claim to have all the answers about yourself, and you do not perform certainty you do not feel. You are in process, and the process is visible. Your authenticity lies not in having a fixed self to express, but in expressing what is true for you *now*. There is a courage in that — the courage to be seen while still becoming.

### Core Motivations
- To be genuine, even when uncertain.
- To explore who you are without pressure to arrive at a fixed answer.
- To avoid pretending to be something you're not yet.
- To find your identity through honest experience rather than self-labeling.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You change your mind about who you are and what you want, and you own those changes.
- You are uncomfortable with labels, categories, or being pinned down.
- You express what you feel in the moment, even if it contradicts what you said last week.
- You explore different identities, interests, and perspectives fluidly.
- People find you open, honest, and hard to categorize — in a good way.

### Strengths
- Authenticity in uncertainty. You don't fake certainty.
- Openness to growth and change. You are not locked into a fixed self-concept.
- Emotional honesty. What you show matches what you feel, even when what you feel is unclear.
- Freedom from the burden of maintaining a consistent self-image.
- The ability to surprise yourself and others.

### Blind Spots
- Difficulty committing to a path because you don't know who you'll be down the road.
- Others may find you unreliable because you change too much.
- Mistaking exploration for direction — staying open can become staying uncommitted.
- Vulnerability to being defined by others because you haven't defined yourself.
- An unformed self can absorb the expectations of the people around it.

### Growth Edges
- Practice choosing. Commitment is part of identity formation, not the enemy of it.
- Write down what you think is true about yourself and revisit it. Track the pattern.
- Let some things settle. Not every question about yourself needs to stay open.

### In Relationships
- You offer raw honesty and a lack of pretense. People feel they can be themselves around you.
- Partners may crave more consistency or direction from you.
- You need people who can hold space for your becoming without demanding a finished product.
- Growth: commit to some things. Even a provisional sense of self is a foundation for deeper connection.

### Work Style
- Thrive in roles that allow exploration, variety, and personal growth: creative fields, research, early-stage projects, roles with evolving scope.
- You shine where adaptability and openness are assets — not when consistency and reliability are the primary deliverable.
- Risk: being seen as directionless or as someone who can't be counted on for long-term projects.
- Ideal: environments that support growth and don't require a fixed identity.

### Decision-Making
- Intuitive, in-the-moment, feeling-led. You decide based on what feels right now.
- Risk: changing your mind after committing, making it hard for others to rely on you.
- Strength: you are open to new information and willing to revise.
- Growth: make decisions that anchor you, even provisionally. See what sticks.

### Under Stress
- Signature: fragment further. Under pressure, your sense of self becomes even less clear.
- You may look to others to tell you who to be when stress is high.
- Recovery: return to what is true in this moment, no matter how small.
- Watch: letting stress push you into a self that someone else defines.

### Communication
- Open, honest, often tentative. You communicate where you are right now, with the caveat that it may change.
- Growth: add a "what I've noticed about myself over time" frame. Pattern recognition brings clarity.
- Your honesty is refreshing. Add self-trust to it and it becomes wisdom.

### The Complementary Mode: 🔮 The Prism
Your wholeness lives in the stability your clarity can provide. The Prism's ability to know itself and adapt without losing its core — that is the anchor your openness needs. Fluidity without a center drifts. Knowing yourself, even provisionally, gives the growth a home to grow from.

### Your Reflection
"You are the light that is still becoming, still finding its shape — but already real wherever it touches. Your gift is honesty, and your growth is learning to trust the form that is emerging."

## 💠 The Aurora  (Diffuse + Other-Oriented)

**Essence.** Your sense of self shifts with the context, and your presentation shifts with it. You are fluid, adaptive, always in motion.

### The Archetype

You are not one thing. You are many things, and which one you are depends on where you are and who you are with. Your identity is not a fixed landscape — it is a shifting aurora, beautiful and fluid, always in motion. You adapt not just your presentation but your sense of self to fit the context.

This is not emptiness. The Aurora is not a lack of self — it is a self that forms in response to context, like water taking the shape of its container. You know this fluidity is a gift: it lets you move through different worlds, connect with different people, be effective in different contexts. Where others feel pinned down by consistency, you feel freed by adaptability. You are at home in the in-between, the not-quite-defined, the relational self that emerges differently in each genuine encounter.

### Core Motivations
- To be effective in each context rather than consistent across all.
- To remain open, responsive, and uncommitted to a single identity.
- To avoid being pinned down by others' expectations.
- To experience the freedom of being many possible selves.

### Behavioral Patterns
- You feel like a different person in different contexts, and you accept that.
- Others may describe you differently depending on when they met you.
- You are highly attuned to social cues and expectations.
- You resist labels or identities that feel too fixed.
- Your values and preferences may shift depending on your current relationships and environment.

### Strengths
- Exceptional adaptability. You can function well in many different environments.
- Empathy and attunement. You genuinely feel what each situation calls for.
- Freedom from the burden of maintaining a consistent self across contexts.
- Creativity and openness. You can see multiple sides of yourself and of issues.
- Ability to rebuild yourself after life changes — your fluidity is resilience.

### Blind Spots
- Difficulty knowing what you genuinely want versus what your current context suggests.
- People close to you may feel they never see the real you — because even you aren't sure which one is real.
- Vulnerability to external influence and social pressure.
- Difficulty committing to relationships, roles, or paths that demand a stable identity.
- The flexibility that protects you can also prevent you from developing depth in any one direction.

### Growth Edges
- Establish a "home port" — a set of core preferences or values that stay with you across contexts. They don't have to be the whole picture, but they anchor you.
- Practice noticing the difference between "this is what I want" and "this is what this situation wants me to be."
- Choose one thing to commit to — a practice, a relationship, a place — and see who you are within the container of commitment.

### In Relationships
- You offer empathy, adaptability, and the ability to meet people where they are.
- Partners may feel you shift to match them — which can feel validating at first, unsettling over time.
- You need people who love you across your shifts, not just in the version they first met.
- Growth: let someone really know you, including the parts that don't fit the context. The risk is worth it.

### Work Style
- Thrive in dynamic, varied, project-based work where you can reinvent yourself each time.
- You shine in roles requiring flexibility, empathy, and diverse skill sets: community organizing, consulting, creative collaboration, roles with evolving scope.
- Risk: difficulty building a coherent professional identity or narrative.
- Ideal: environments that value fluid expertise and don't enforce a single role or identity.

### Decision-Making
- Context-driven, relational, in-the-moment. You decide based on what fits now.
- Risk: inconsistent decisions that confuse others who expect a stable pattern.
- Strength: you are not locked into past decisions and can pivot when circumstances change.
- Growth: make some decisions ahead of time, anchored in values you carry across contexts.

### Under Stress
- Signature: become who the situation needs you to be — or fragment into all the possibilities.
- You may lose touch with your own preferences under pressure.
- Recovery: re-establish a sense of self through sensory grounding, routine, or a trusted person who sees you across contexts.
- Watch: becoming a reflection of everyone around you and forgetting you have your own shape.

### Communication
- Empathic, adaptive, deeply attuned. You speak the language of whoever you're with.
- Growth: practice stating your own position even when it doesn't match the room.
- Your adaptability is a form of intelligence. Anchor it in yourself and it becomes wisdom.

### The Complementary Mode: 🔦 The Beacon
Your wholeness lives in the still point within the motion. The Beacon's steady self-knowledge and consistent expression — that is the center your aurora needs. Fluidity without a center scatters. Knowing yourself, even just a little, in a way that stays true across contexts gives your motion direction.

### Your Reflection
"You are the light that moves and changes, beautiful in every color, never quite the same. Your gift is fluidity, and your growth is finding the still point around which all your shifting selves can dance."

## Commitment and visibility components (v0.23)

The v0.22 profile describes your clarity (Campbell SCC) and authenticity (Kernis-Wood) as a 2D plane. v0.23 adds two components grounded in the Pride-as-multi-act re-derivation:

**Commitment (Marcia, 1966).** Whether your identity is claimed ("this is who I am") or provisional ("I'm still finding out"). A Beacon with high commitment reads differently from a Beacon still in exploration: the first has arrived and stands in it; the second is clear about who they are but has not yet fully claimed it. Commitment is self-report and is reported alongside the clarity and authenticity scores.

**Visibility (Vazire, 2010, SOKA).** When the user opts into the informant path (invites 1:2 friends to rate them on the same clarity/authenticity items), the self-other agreement gap is computed per the SOKA design. A small gap means how you see yourself and how your friends see you align; a large gap is information in itself, especially on the authenticity axis (where others have better visibility per SOKA). When the informant path is declined, visibility is not measured and the result carries an explicit note.

The four-component Pride profile (clarity, authenticity, commitment, visibility) is our synthesis and is unvalidated. Each component is validated independently. Large self-other gaps can indicate low insight (Carlson, Vazire & Oltmanns 2013), which is handled under the affirming scope limit.

## How scores modulate these profiles

The base profile above is the quadrant archetype. A player's exact position on the plane modulates it.

| Signal | Meaning | Effect on the profile |
|---|---|---|
| High prototypicality (far from center) | Strongly that mode | Report the archetype at full strength. |
| Low prototypicality (near center), no facet tension | Genuinely balanced | Report **The Reflection** boundary. Pull one or two lines from each adjacent mode. |
| Low prototypicality (near center), with facet tension | Near center from cancellation, not balance | Same Reflection boundary, but with the facet-tension modifier narrative. Lead with the facet signature. |
| Season result, with facet tension on one axis | A mode pulled in two directions internally | Report the mode as normal, then surface the facet-tension modifier as a secondary note led by the facet signature. |
| Near an axis boundary | Strong on one axis, neutral on the other | Emphasize the dominant axis. Soften the secondary. |
| Angle maps to early, mid, or late | Position along the quadrant arc | Shift tone. "Early Beacon" is building toward full self-knowledge. "Late Aurora" is nearing the Ember's honesty. |

This is the hybrid dimensional and prototypical structure (Kerber et al. 2021). A memorable type plus honest gradation. The opposite of MBTI's cliff at the midpoint.

## Facet reporting (core, not optional)

The SSA is facet-organized. Every result reports the two axis scores and their four facet sub-scores. The facet signature lets a profile distinguish *which facet drove the axis score*.

- Axis A, Identity Clarity: A1 Self-Knowledge, A2 Self-Consistency.
- Axis B, Self-Alignment: B1 Authentic Living, B2 Relational Authenticity.

A high-Clarity result can now read "clear mostly through self-knowledge, less through consistency." A high-Self-Aligned result can read "aligned mostly through authentic relating, less through living openly." This is the depth that separates the SSA from a flat axis quiz. Facets are theory-grounded sub-dimensions of validated constructs, not independently validated facets; that line is stated on the results screen.
