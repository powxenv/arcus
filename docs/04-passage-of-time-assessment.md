# Test 4: The Passage of Time Assessment (PTA)

> Status: v0.22 design document. Redesigned from the v0.21 2-axis plane. The previous ZTPI-composite axis, the self-continuity axis (which collided with SSA's Self-Consistency), the Narrative Coherence designer facet, the fullness-tension logic, and the Archivist/Navigator/Storyteller/Pioneer quadrant names are all superseded. The v0.22 PTA is a validated 3-factor instrument with 8 temporal types. Each factor is one full-text-verified construct in its native unipolar agree-disagree format. See CHANGELOG v0.22.
> Position: The passage-of-time-themed test of the four-test Solstice Soul suite.
> Theme served: Passage of Time (how a person relates to time passing).
> Honesty statement: The PTA is an original, theory-informed assessment for reflection and play. Its three factors are grounded in full-text-verified constructs: Past-Positive engagement draws from ZTPI Past-Positive (Zimbardo & Boyd, 1999) and Webster's (1993) adaptive reminiscence functions; Present-eudaimonic engagement draws from Vowinckel et al. (2015); Future engagement draws from ZTPI Future and the Future-Positive factor (Stolarski & Matthews, 2016). It is not independently validated and is not clinical. It measures the affirming range of time perspective only; Past-Negative (rumination), Present-Fatalistic (hopelessness), and Future-Negative (dread) are excluded by the suite's binding affirming scope limit (see `00-suite-framework.md` §3) and cannot be detected.

---

## 1. The name

**The Passage of Time Assessment (PTA).**

"Passage of Time" names the theme. "Assessment" matches the suite convention. The in-game UI may use "The Time Compass" as a friendlier label.

## 2. Theoretical foundation (each source verified against full-text)

### 2.1 Structural sources

- **Zimbardo and Boyd (1999), Zimbardo Time Perspective Inventory.** Time perspective as "the often nonconscious process whereby the continual flows of personal and social experiences are assigned to temporal categories" (p. 1271). Five factors: Past-Positive, Past-Negative, Present-Hedonistic, Present-Fatalistic, Future. *Use:* core anchor for Factors 1 and 3 (Past-Positive, Future).

- **Sircova et al. (2014), 24-country validation (N = 12,200).** Confirms the five-factor structure cross-culturally with 36 invariant items. Subscale alphas: Past-Negative .77, Present-Hedonistic .69, Past-Positive .66, Future .64, Present-Fatalistic .60. Table 4 latent-factor correlations drive the central honesty note (§2.5): Past-Positive ↔ Past-Negative r = −.20; Future ↔ Past-Positive r = +.26; Future ↔ Past-Negative r = −.06; Present-Fatalistic ↔ Past-Negative r = +.58. *Use:* establishes the factors are distinct and cross-culturally stable, and establishes that the affirming factors (Past-Positive, Future) are only weakly related, which is why each is scored independently rather than composited.

- **Vowinckel, Westerhof, Bohlmeijer and Webster (2015), present-eudaimonic time perspective.** Built and validated a positive-present scale precisely because ZTPI lacks one: its two present factors (Present-Hedonistic, Present-Fatalistic) are not unequivocally positive. The present-eudaimonic scale explains an additional 11 percent of variance in mental health beyond other time-perspective scales. *Use:* core anchor for Factor 2 (Present-eudaimonic engagement). This is the validated construct that lets the PTA treat present-focus as a first-class result rather than an exile.

- **Stolarski and Matthews (2016), incremental validity over the Big Five.** Time perspective predicts an additional 13.7 percent of variance in life satisfaction beyond the Big Five. Documents the six-factor extension (Carelli et al.) splitting Future into Future-Positive and Future-Negative, and reports Future ↔ Conscientiousness r = .60. *Use:* grounds Factor 3 (Future engagement built from the positive factor) and the cross-suite overlap honesty note (Future loads on Conscientiousness).

- **Webster (1993), Reminiscence Functions Scale.** Seven self-report functions of thinking about the past: Boredom Reduction, Death Preparation, Identity/Problem-Solving, Conversation, Intimacy Maintenance, Bitterness Revival, Teach/Inform. *Use:* content basis for Factor 1. The adaptive functions (Identity/Problem-Solving, Teach/Inform, Death Preparation) inform the rooted pole; Bitterness Revival and Boredom Reduction are explicitly excluded so the factor stays affirming.

- **Westerhof, Bohlmeijer and Webster (2010), reminiscence and mental health review.** Maps reminiscence functions to mental health and lifespan. *Use:* confirms which functions are adaptive versus maladaptive, guiding Factor 1 content so that low past engagement reads as "focused elsewhere," not "stuck in bitterness."

- **Sobol-Kwapinska and Jankowski (2015), balanced time perspective and positive orientation.** Links balanced time perspective to positive orientation and argues for an active-concentration (Carpe Diem) present beyond hedonism and fatalism. *Use:* corroboration for the affirming present and for the Balanced type (Factor 7).

### 2.2 Sources rejected after full-text review

- **Hall (1959); Schell and Conte (2008); Bluedorn (1999), monochronic/polychronic time.** Operational task-organization construct, validated primarily in work and cross-cultural settings. Confounds with future orientation and with Conscientiousness. Does not answer "how do you relate to time." Rejected as a structural axis in v0.21; rejection confirmed in v0.22.

- **Strathman et al. (1994); Joireman et al. (2012), Consideration of Future Consequences.** Decision-theoretic (how far ahead you weigh consequences), not experiential. Would reduce the theme to a planning-horizon instrument. Rejected.

- **McAdams et al. (2001); Adler et al. (2015), narrative identity (redemption, contamination, agency, communion).** Real and consequential, but coded from open-ended life-story interviews with no validated Likert equivalent. Same measurement-format wall that rejected Showers (1992) and Linville (1987) in Test 3. In v0.22, narrative identity is profile texture only, not a scored axis or facet.

- **Hershfield et al. (2009), future self-continuity.** Validated (future-self-only) self-report construct. Considered as a fourth factor in v0.22. Not adopted, because (a) the past-self extension used in v0.21 collided item-for-item with SSA's Self-Consistency facet, and (b) the future-self signal is largely captured by Factor 3 (Future engagement). The verified construct is noted here; it is not used structurally so that PTA and SSA measure different things.

### 2.3 Response-format sources (decided per construct, not per suite)

The PTA uses **5-point agree-disagree unipolar Likert**, the native validated format of the ZTPI and the present-eudaimonic scale. This is a deliberate departure from the 7-point bipolar format used in SCA and SSA. Those constructs (activation, identity clarity) are genuinely bipolar; the ZTPI factors are unipolar. Forcing a bipolar format onto unipolar factors creates artificial antonyms and composites weakly-related poles. Point count follows the source instrument.

- **Chang (1994).** Five-point Likert is psychometrically adequate with strong discrimination and low cognitive load.
- **Sun, Schmidt & Henry (2025).** Reject sliders.
- **McCrae & Costa (1989); Schulte et al. (2020).** Reject dichotomizing and forced-choice.
- **Spearman-Brown.** Eight items per factor projects expected α .85 to .92; four items per factor (the short form) lands in the .60 to .70 screening tier.

### 2.5 Facet grounding and honesty notes

**The three factors are independent, not composited.** This is the defining change from v0.21. Sircova's Table 4 shows Future ↔ Past-Positive r = +.26 (weakly related), and the present-eudaimonic factor is largely independent of both (Vowinckel et al. 2015). The three factors are scored 0 to 100 each and reported independently. There is no composite axis, because compositing weakly-related factors was the core error of v0.21.

**Honesty note 1: Each factor measures engagement, not valence-free orientation.** Past-Positive engagement is warmth toward roots, not "past orientation" in general (which would include rumination). Present-eudaimonic is active meaningful presence, not "present orientation" in general (which would include fatalism and hedonism). Future engagement is anticipatory pull toward possibility, not "future orientation" in general (which would include dread). The affirming scope limit (00 §3) excludes the negative-valence half of each temporal zone.

**Honesty note 2: Past-Negative, Present-Fatalistic, and Future-Negative are not measured.** These are the strongest predictors of low well-being in the time-perspective literature (Stolarski & Matthews 2016; Sircova et al. 2014). The PTA cannot detect rumination, hopelessness, or anxious dread about the future. A result that reads as affirming does not rule these out. This is the binding scope limit, stated once in 00 §3 and surfaced on every result screen.

**Honesty note 3: The "no zone engaged" reading (The Wanderer) is affirming on the surface and clinical underneath.** A respondent low on all three factors receives The Wanderer reading (unbound, free). Low positive time-perspective across all three zones does correlate with lower well-being in the literature. The affirming surface is honest about being a surface; the scope note carries the clinical caveat. The PTA does not claim to distinguish "genuinely free and unbound" from "disengaged in a way that warrants attention."

**Honesty note 4: Present-eudaimonic is the newest and least-validated factor.** ZTPI (1999) has 25 years of cross-cultural validation. Vowinckel's present-eudaimonic scale (2015) is validated but younger and less widely replicated. Factor 2 is therefore the highest-variance of the three. Stated on the result screen for present-leaning and balanced results.

**Honesty note 5: Future engagement loads on Conscientiousness.** Stolarski & Matthews (2016) report ZTPI Future ↔ Conscientiousness r = .60. Factor 3 therefore shares substantial variance with MMA's override diligence and SSA's authenticity. This is stated in the cross-suite shared-variance map (00 §9), not hidden.

## 3. Why the framework fits the Passage of Time theme

Time is the medium a life moves through. The PTA measures how a person engages with the three temporal zones of that medium: the past they came from, the present they live in, and the future they move toward. The three-factor structure is the construct's own shape: time perspective is multi-factor (ZTPI's five factors, confirmed cross-culturally), and under the affirming scope limit, three of those factors are usable as positive poles. Forcing them into two axes (v0.21) composited weakly-related factors and exiled the present. The v0.22 structure lets each zone stand on its own.

The test's framing: *Time has three rooms: the one you came from, the one you're in, and the one you're walking toward. Which ones are lit?*

## 4. The dimensions measured

Three independent factors, each 0 to 100.

**Factor 1, Past-Positive engagement.** Warm, rooted relationship with where you have been: roots, memory, heritage, the people and places that shaped you.
- Construct basis: ZTPI Past-Positive (Zimbardo & Boyd 1999; Sircova et al. 2014) + Webster (1993) adaptive reminiscence functions.
- High: you feel connected to your roots and draw meaning from your history.
- Low: your attention is elsewhere; the past is not a strong pull.

**Factor 2, Present-eudaimonic engagement.** Active, meaningful engagement with the now: flow, presence, appreciation of the ordinary.
- Construct basis: Vowinckel et al. (2015) present-eudaimonic time perspective.
- High: you are genuinely here, engaged with what is in front of you.
- Low: the present is something you move through, not somewhere you live.

**Factor 3, Future engagement.** Anticipatory pull toward goals, possibility, and what will be.
- Construct basis: ZTPI Future (Zimbardo & Boyd 1999) + Future-Positive factor (Stolarski & Matthews 2016, citing Carelli et al.).
- High: the future pulls you; you are oriented toward possibility.
- Low: the future is not your compass; you are not chasing a horizon.

The three factors are scored independently. There is no composite axis and no facet-tension modifier (independent factors cannot cancel).

## 5. The eight temporal types

Types are derived from which factor(s) clear the engagement threshold (≥ 60). The threshold is designer-set and tunable; with pilot data it should be calibrated to the distribution.

| Pattern | Type | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| Past ≥ 60, others < 60 | 📜 **The Keeper** | Preserves and honors roots. The past is alive and grounding. |
| Present ≥ 60, others < 60 | ⏳ **The Witness** | Lives fully in the now. The present is rich and enough. |
| Future ≥ 60, others < 60 | 🧭 **The Wayfinder** | Moves toward horizons. The future pulls. |
| Past + Present ≥ 60, Future < 60 | 🕯️ **The Hearth** | Roots warming the living moment. Memory feeds presence. |
| Past + Future ≥ 60, Present < 60 | 🌉 **The Bridge** | Spans from roots to horizons. The present is the crossing. |
| Present + Future ≥ 60, Past < 60 | 🌊 **The Flow** | Moves forward, fully present. Now and next are one motion. |
| All three ≥ 60 | ⚖️ **Balanced Time Perspective** | All of time engaged. The validated BTP construct (Zimbardo & Boyd; Sobol-Kwapinska & Jankowski 2015). |
| None ≥ 60 | 🍃 **The Wanderer** | Unbound by any zone. Free-floating. (Affirming surface; see honesty note 3.) |

Full profiles live in `04b-ptoa-result-profiles.md`.

The type is the interpretive label. The measurement is the three continuous factor scores, always reported alongside. A respondent near a threshold (e.g., Past 58, Present 59) is between types, and the result surfaces the dimensional scores and the near-miss rather than forcing a clean label.

## 6. Methodology and scoring

**Step 1, collect.** 24 responses (8 per factor), each 1 to 5.

**Step 2, direction-normalize.** All items are written so that "agree" means more engagement. No reverse-keying needed in the v0.22 bank (all items are engagement-positive statements).

**Step 3, factor scores.** Each factor = mean of its 8 items, scaled 0 to 100: `((mean − 1) / 4) × 100`.

**Step 4, type assignment.** Apply the engagement threshold (≥ 60) to each factor independently. The pattern of high/low across the three factors selects one of the eight types. If a respondent sits within ±5 of the threshold on any factor, surface a "between types" note with the dimensional scores.

**Step 5, prototypicality.** For single-zone types, prototypicality = how far the engaged factor is above 60 and how far the other two are below. For multi-zone types, prototypicality = the mean distance of the engaged factors above 60. Low prototypicality means the type is a soft fit; the dimensional scores do the real work.

There is no facet-tension modifier and no boundary condition. Independent factors do not cancel, and the multi-zone types are first-class results, not a midpoint compromise.

## 7. Question format and response scale

**5-point agree-disagree unipolar Likert, statement format.**

Each item presents a single engagement-positive statement and asks the respondent to rate their agreement from 1 (Strongly disagree) to 5 (Strongly agree). This is the native validated format of the ZTPI and the Vowinckel present-eudaimonic scale.

This is a deliberate departure from SCA and SSA, which use 7-point bipolar Likert. Those constructs (activation, identity clarity, authenticity) are genuinely bipolar; the ZTPI factors are unipolar. Forcing a bipolar format onto unipolar factors creates artificial antonyms (e.g., "I love my roots" vs "I focus on the future" are not opposites; they are independent). The PTA asks one clean engagement question per item.

## 8. Item bank and lengths

**Two lengths, one item bank.** Standard form: 24 questions (~6 to 8 minutes). Quick form: 12 questions (~3 to 4 minutes), 4 items per factor, screening-tier (Spearman-Brown α ≈ .60 to .70).

**Item-writing rules.** Each item varies exactly one factor. Engagement-positive phrasing only. Neutral language. No transparent giveaways. Age-agnostic and gender-neutral. The low-agreement pole reads as "my attention is elsewhere," never as deficit.

### Factor 1: Past-Positive engagement (8 items)

All items vary warm, rooted relationship with the past. Drawn from ZTPI Past-Positive and Webster's (1993) adaptive reminiscence functions. No bitterness or regret (excluded by the affirming scope limit).

| # | Statement |
|---|---|
| P1.1 | I feel a real connection to where I come from. |
| P1.2 | Memories of meaningful moments from my past bring me warmth. |
| P1.3 | The traditions and rituals I grew up with still carry meaning for me. |
| P1.4 | I feel gratitude when I think about the people and places that shaped me. |
| P1.5 | Looking back at how I became who I am, I see a thread worth honoring. |
| P1.6 | I enjoy revisiting memories from earlier in my life. |
| P1.7 | The stories, music, or art from my past still feel like part of who I am. |
| P1.8 | My roots are a real and living part of my identity. |

### Factor 2: Present-eudaimonic engagement (8 items)

All items vary active, meaningful engagement with the present. Drawn from Vowinckel et al. (2015). Not hedonism (Present-Hedonistic) and not fatalism (Present-Fatalistic).

| # | Statement |
|---|---|
| PR2.1 | I feel genuinely engaged with whatever I'm doing right now. |
| PR2.2 | The present moment feels rich and full to me. |
| PR2.3 | I find meaning in ordinary, everyday experiences. |
| PR2.4 | I'm able to be fully present with the people I'm with. |

| PR2.7 | I feel alive in the moment, not just waiting for what's next. |
| PR2.8 | Being fully here, now, comes naturally to me. |

### Factor 3: Future engagement (8 items)

All items vary anticipatory pull toward goals and possibility. Drawn from ZTPI Future and the Future-Positive factor. No Future-Negative (dread).

| # | Statement |
|---|---|
| F3.1 | I feel pulled toward the years ahead of me. |
| F3.2 | Having goals to work toward is important to how I live. |
| F3.3 | Imagining who I could become feels vivid and motivating. |
| F3.4 | When I make choices, I think about where they'll lead. |
| F3.5 | Hopes and dreams for the future shape how I live day to day. |
| F3.6 | The sense that good things may lie ahead excites me. |
| F3.7 | A good deal of my energy goes into what's coming. |
| F3.8 | The future feels alive and full of possibility for me. |

**Notes.** All 24 items are age-agnostic and gender-neutral. All items are engagement-positive; disagreement reads as "my attention is elsewhere," not as deficit. A launch pass should run an internal-consistency check per factor and drop or replace the weakest 1 to 2 items if any factor alpha falls below .75.

## 9. Interpretation and reporting

A result has three layers, surfaced in order:
1. **Your temporal type** (one of eight). The shareable label.
2. **Your three factor scores** (Past, Present, Future, each 0 to 100). The measurement.
3. **Your narrative.** Two to three personalized paragraphs, ending with a "your relationship to time" line. Gemini layer per the GDD, with a deterministic fallback.

**Deterministic fallback (no-API path).** 8 base type profiles (see `04b-ptoa-result-profiles.md`), modulated by prototypicality and by which factors drove the type. Fully playable without the API.

**Scope note on every result.** "This assessment measures the affirming range of time perspective and cannot detect rumination, hopelessness, or anxious dread about the future, which may warrant clinical attention."

**Never reported as:** a maturity score, a diagnostic, a fixed destiny, or a clinical assessment. Always framed as a snapshot of how you currently engage with the three temporal zones.

## 10. Strengths, limitations, intended use

**Strengths.**
- Three independent validated constructs, each in its native unipolar format. No composites, no destroyed orthogonality.
- Present-focus is a first-class result (The Witness), not an exile. This fixes the central flaw of the v0.21 design, which had no validated positive-present factor and exiled present-oriented respondents to a boundary.
- No collision with SSA. The v0.21 self-continuity axis is gone; PTA and SSA now measure different things.
- Affirming across all eight types, including The Wanderer (with an explicit scope note for the clinical underside).
- Balanced Time Perspective is the validated construct name (Zimbardo & Boyd; Sobol-Kwapinska & Jankowski 2015), not an invented label.

**Limitations.**
- Not a validated clinical instrument. Inspired by validated models. Not independently psychometrically evaluated.
- Measures the affirming range only (00 §3). Past-Negative, Present-Fatalistic, Future-Negative excluded. The PTA cannot detect rumination, hopelessness, or dread.
- Present-eudaimonic (Factor 2) is the youngest and least-replicated of the three constructs (Vowinckel et al. 2015). Highest-variance factor.
- The threshold-based type assignment (≥ 60) is designer-set and uncalibrated. With pilot data it should be calibrated to the actual distribution.
- Future engagement shares substantial variance with Conscientiousness (Stolarski & Matthews 2016: r = .60) and therefore with MMA and SSA.
- Time perspective is partly state. Results shift with life circumstances, age, and perceived endings (Carstensen 2021).

**Intended use.** Reflective self-discovery and play within the Solstice Soul game.

**Explicitly not intended for.** Clinical diagnosis, retirement planning, employment decisions, or any high-stakes real-world application.
