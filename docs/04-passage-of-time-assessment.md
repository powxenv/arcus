# Test 4: The Passage of Time Assessment (PTA)

> Status: v0.1 design document. Full-text paper audit of 16 time/temporality PDFs. Two axes grounded in full-text-verified constructs: Temporal Direction (Zimbardo & Boyd 1999 ZTPI; Sircova et al. 2014 24-country validation; Stolarski & Matthews 2016 incremental validity) and Temporal Mode (Hershfield et al. 2009 temporal self-continuity; McAdams et al. 2001 and Adler et al. 2015 narrative identity). Monochronic/Polychronic time rejected as a structural axis: operational task-organization construct, Axis-1 confound, Conscientiousness confound. Consideration of Future Consequences rejected: decision-theoretic, not experiential. Narrative identity rejected as a structural axis in its interview-coded form: format incompatible with self-report, same standard as Showers/Linville in Test 3. Present-gap acknowledged (Vowinckel et al. 2015). 32-item bank. See CHANGELOG v0.1.
> Position: The passage-of-time-themed test of the four-test Solstice Soul suite.
> Theme served: Passage of Time (how a person relates to time passing: where they face, and how they move through it).
> Honesty statement: The PTA is an original, theory-informed assessment for reflection and play. Its two axes are grounded in full-text-verified constructs: Temporal Direction draws from the Zimbardo Time Perspective Inventory (Zimbardo & Boyd, 1999) and its 24-country validation (Sircova et al., 2014); Temporal Mode draws from temporal self-continuity research (Hershfield et al., 2009; Hershfield, 2011) and the narrative-identity tradition (McAdams et al., 2001; Adler et al., 2015). It is not independently validated and is not clinical. The four result names are an interpretive metaphor drawn from journey and time imagery: how you move through time as a self. Self-report is the measurement format; its limitations for temporal constructs are stated in §10.

---

## 1. The name

**The Passage of Time Assessment (PTA).**

"Passage of Time" names the theme directly. "Assessment" matches the suite convention (SCA, MMA, SSA). The framework name is PTA.

The in-game UI may use "The Time Compass" as a friendlier label, but the framework name is PTA.

## 2. Theoretical foundation (each source states its use, each verified against full-text)

Sources below are retained because full-text PDFs confirm the claimed use.

### 2.1 Structural sources (define what we measure)

- **Zimbardo and Boyd (1999), The Zimbardo Time Perspective Inventory (JPSP).** Defines time perspective as "the often nonconscious process whereby the continual flows of personal and social experiences are assigned to temporal categories, or time frames" (p. 1271). The ZTPI has five factors: Past-Positive, Past-Negative, Present-Hedonistic, Present-Fatalistic, and Future. *Use:* core anchor for Axis 1 (Temporal Direction). The Past-Positive and Future factors ground the two poles. The full five-factor structure is not adopted (see §2.5 honesty note 1).

- **Sircova et al. (2014), A Global Look at the Time (SAGE Open).** Assesses the structural equivalence of the ZTPI across 26 samples from 24 countries (N = 12,200). Confirms the five-factor structure holds cross-culturally with 36 invariant items. Mean subscale alphas: Past-Negative .77, Present-Hedonistic .69, Past-Positive .66, Future .64, Present-Fatalistic .60. *Use:* establishes that time perspective is a robust, cross-culturally stable individual difference, not a Western artifact. Its Table 4 latent-factor correlation matrix (23-country, n = 10,765) drives the honesty note on compositing (§2.5): Past-Positive ↔ Past-Negative r = −.20, Future ↔ Past-Positive r = +.26, Future ↔ Past-Negative r = −.06, Present-Fatalistic ↔ Past-Negative r = +.58.

- **Stolarski and Matthews (2016), Time Perspectives Predict Mood States and Satisfaction with Life over and above Personality.** Tests the incremental validity of time perspective over the Big Five in predicting life satisfaction and mood (N = 265). Time perspective predicts an additional 13.7 percent of variance in life satisfaction beyond the Big Five. Confirms that Future correlates r = .60 with Conscientiousness, Past-Negative with Neuroticism. Documents the six-factor extension (Carelli et al.) splitting Future into Future-Positive and Future-Negative. *Use:* grounds two honesty notes: that time perspective is genuine variance not reducible to personality, and that Axis 1 shares substantial variance with Conscientiousness and therefore with Test 2.

- **Hershfield, Garton, Ballard, Samanez-Larkin, and Knutson (2009), Don't Stop Thinking About Tomorrow (Judgment and Decision Making).** Develops and validates a self-report index of future self-continuity: the perceived connection between the present self and a future self. Four seven-point Likert items (similarity, connectedness, caring, liking). Test-retest reliabilities range from r = .45 to r = .66 (α = .62 to .80). Higher future self-continuity predicts reduced temporal discounting and greater lifetime saving, controlling for age and education. *Use:* core anchor for Axis 2, facet B1 (Temporal Self-Continuity). The construct extends naturally to the past self (Sani, 2010); the PTA measures continuity across both directions.

- **Hershfield (2011), Future Self-Continuity (Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences).** Synthesizes the self-continuity construct and its behavioral consequences. *Use:* corroboration and construct elaboration for Axis 2. Pairs with the 2009 paper to establish that perceived temporal continuity of the self is a measurable, consequential individual difference.

- **McAdams, Reynolds, Lewis, Patten, and Bowman (2001), Redemption and Contamination Sequences (Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin).** Defines two core narrative sequences: redemption (a bad scene gives way to a good one; the bad is redeemed) and contamination (a good scene is spoiled by what follows). Redemption sequences predict higher well-being; contamination predicts lower well-being, and redemption predicts well-being more strongly than overall narrative affect. *Use:* construct basis for Axis 2, facet B2 (Narrative Coherence). The original measurement is coded from open-ended life-story interviews; the PTA uses a self-report adaptation, stated as a designer facet (§2.5 honesty note 4).

- **Adler, Turner, Brookshier, Monroe, and colleagues (2015), Variation in Narrative Identity (Journal of Research in Personality).** Two longitudinal studies showing that narrative themes (agency, communion, redemption, contamination) measured at one time point predict trajectories of mental health over years. *Use:* establishes that the narrative-identity constructs grounding B2 are consequential, not merely descriptive. Strengthens the case for a self-report adaptation, with the format limitation stated.

### 2.2 Facet-content sources (texture for the past and present poles)

- **Webster (1993), Construction and Validation of the Reminiscence Functions Scale (The Gerontologist).** Develops a 43-item self-report instrument yielding seven reminiscence functions: Boredom Reduction, Death Preparation, Identity/Problem-Solving, Conversation, Intimacy Maintenance, Bitterness Revival, and Teach/Inform. *Use:* content basis for the A1 (Past Engagement) item bank. The adaptive functions (Identity/Problem-Solving, Teach/Inform, Death Preparation) inform the rooted pole; Bitterness Revival and Boredom Reduction are explicitly excluded so the past pole stays affirming.

- **Westerhof, Bohlmeijer, and Webster (2010), Reminiscence and Mental Health (Ageing & Society).** Reviews theory and evidence mapping reminiscence functions to mental health and lifespan development. *Use:* confirms which reminiscence functions are adaptive versus maladaptive, guiding pole design so the low and high poles of A1 are both affirming.

- **Vowinckel, Westerhof, Bohlmeijer, and Webster (2015), Flourishing in the Now (Time & Society).** Develops and validates a present-eudaimonic time perspective scale, arguing that the ZTPI lacks a positive-present factor: its two present factors are Present-Hedonistic (linked in some studies to risk behavior) and Present-Fatalistic (negative). The present-eudaimonic scale explains an additional 11 percent of variance in mental health beyond other time-perspective scales. *Use:* the basis for the present-gap honesty note (§2.5) and for treating present-oriented respondents as the boundary condition with affirming texture, rather than as a deficient or exiled group.

- **Sobol-Kwapinska and Jankowski (2015), Positive Time: Balanced Time Perspective and Positive Orientation.** Argues for a third present orientation (active concentration, the Carpe Diem scale) beyond hedonism and fatalism, and links balanced time perspective to positive orientation. *Use:* corroboration for the affirming reframing of present orientation in the boundary condition.

### 2.3 Alternative-axis sources rejected after full-text review

- **Hall (1959); Schell and Conte (2008); Bluedorn (1999), Polychronicity and the Inventory of Polychronic Values.** Monochronicity (clock-time, one-thing-at-a-time, scheduled) versus polychronicity (event-time, multiple flows, contextual). Validated as a stable individual difference, primarily in work and organizational settings and in cross-cultural research. *Use:* rejected as a structural axis. Three reasons. First, it is an operational task-organization construct rather than an experiential relationship with time passing; it does not answer "how do you move through time." Second, it confounds with Axis 1 (the framework itself documents that monochronicity correlates with future orientation). Third, it confounds with Conscientiousness (Stolarski & Matthews, 2016: Future ↔ Conscientiousness r = .60). A self-report mono/poly instrument would re-measure variance already carried by Axis 1 and by Test 2.

- **Strathman, Gleicher, Boninger, and colleagues (1994); Joireman, Shaffer, Balliet, and colleagues (2012), Consideration of Future Consequences (CFC).** A two-factor scale (CFC-Future, CFC-Immediate) measuring how far ahead a person weighs the consequences of current behavior. *Use:* rejected as a structural axis. CFC is decision-theoretic: it measures a decision horizon, not a relationship with time as a lived dimension. It would reduce the poetic passage-of-time theme to a planning-horizon instrument and break tonal parity with Tests 1 to 3.

- **McAdams and Adler narrative identity in its canonical form.** The redemption, contamination, agency, and communion themes are real, consequential, and temporally central. But they are coded from open-ended life-story interviews. There is no validated Likert equivalent. This is the same measurement-format wall that rejected Showers (1992) and Linville (1987) in Test 3. Narrative identity is used as the construct basis for facet B2 via a self-report adaptation, not as a structural axis. The adaptation is a designer choice, stated openly (§2.5 honesty note 4).

### 2.4 Response-format sources (inherit from Test 1)

Decisions apply unchanged from Test 1. Full argument in `01-solstice-cycle-assessment.md` §7.
- **Sun, Schmidt and Henry (2025).** Sliders underperform Likert.
- **Chang (1994), Gosling et al. (2003), Spearman-Brown.** Seven points, eight items per facet.
- **McCrae and Costa (1989), Schulte et al. (2020).** Reject forced-choice and dichotomizing.

### 2.5 Facet grounding and honesty notes

**Axis 1, Temporal Direction.** Where your attention and value sit across time: anchored in the past or oriented toward the future.

- **A1 Past Engagement:** grounded in ZTPI Past-Positive and Webster's (1993) adaptive reminiscence functions. The degree to which you relate warmly and meaningfully to your roots, memory, heritage, and where you have been. Items ask about felt connection to the past, not about rumination or bitterness (those are excluded by design).
- **A2 Future Engagement:** grounded in ZTPI Future and the Future-Positive factor (Stolarski & Matthews, 2016, citing Carelli et al.). The degree to which you are pulled toward goals, possibility, and what will be. Items ask about anticipatory engagement, not anxiety about the future.

**Honesty note 1: Axis 1 is a composite of two separable constructs.** Past-Positive and Future are distinct ZTPI factors and are only weakly correlated (Sircova et al., 2014, Table 4: Future ↔ Past-Positive r = +.26). They are not opposite poles of one dimension. Combining them into a single Past↔Future axis is a designer simplification. The information loss is recovered as facet sub-scores (A1 and A2 reported separately in every result), so a past-anchored score can distinguish "high past engagement, moderate future" from "low future engagement, moderate past." The axis score itself is a directional summary, not a claim that past and future are a single bipolar trait.

**Honesty note 2: The negative-valence past is not measured.** Past-Negative (bitter rumination, regret) is a real ZTPI factor (Sircova: Past-Negative ↔ Past-Positive r = −.20; Past-Negative ↔ Present-Fatalistic r = +.58) and a strong predictor of low well-being. The PTA deliberately excludes it so the past pole stays affirming. The cost: a past-anchored result does not distinguish "warmly rooted" from "stuck in difficult memories." This is the same affirming-by-design constraint that excluded hubristic pride in Test 3. The distinction is a measurement ceiling the framework states rather than hides.

**Honesty note 3: The ZTPI has no validated positive-present factor.** Vowinckel et al. (2015) built the present-eudaimonic scale precisely because ZTPI's two present factors (Present-Hedonistic, Present-Fatalistic) are not unequivocally positive. A respondent low on both A1 (past) and A2 (future) is genuinely present-focused, but the PTA does not measure present engagement as a validated construct. Present-oriented respondents land near the center of Axis 1 and are read as the boundary condition (The Horizon), affirmatively framed as living in the now. This is a designed reframing, not a validation that the present pole is a distinct, measurable construct.

**Honesty note 4: B2 Narrative Coherence is a self-report adaptation of an interview-coded construct.** McAdams and colleagues code redemption, contamination, agency, and communion from open-ended life-story interviews. There is no validated Likert instrument for these themes. The PTA's B2 items adapt the underlying construct (holding one's life as a coherent, meaningful narrative) into self-report scenarios. This is a designer facet, the same honesty standard as Test 3's A1/A2 designer split. If pilot data shows B1 and B2 converging, the separate reporting is harmless.

**Axis 2, Temporal Mode.** How your self moves through time: as one continuous thread or as a fluid sequence of revisable moments.

- **B1 Temporal Self-Continuity:** Hershfield et al. (2009), Hershfield (2011). The perceived connection between your present self and your past and future selves. High scores mean you experience yourself as the same continuing being across years; low scores mean your self feels more distinct across time.
- **B2 Narrative Coherence:** self-report adaptation of McAdams et al. (2001) and Adler et al. (2015). The degree to which you hold your life as one intelligible, meaningful story rather than as a sequence of distinct episodes. High scores mean the events of your life fit a larger arc; low scores mean each season stands on its own.

**Honesty note 5: Overlap with Test 3 (identity).** Temporal self-continuity is conceptually adjacent to Test 3's identity domain: both concern the self across time. The overlap is real and stated. The distinction is that the SSA measures current identity clarity and expression (how well you know and show who you are now), while the PTA measures temporal extension of the self (how continuous your self feels across past, present, and future). They share variance; the PTA's contribution is the temporal, not the identity-structural, framing.

**Honesty note 6: Overlap with Test 2 (cognition) and Conscientiousness.** Future orientation correlates r = .60 with Conscientiousness (Stolarski & Matthews, 2016), and Test 2's deliberate pole loads on Conscientiousness. Axis 1 therefore shares substantial variance with Test 2. The PTA's contribution is the temporal framing and the facet combination, not a trait independent of Conscientiousness. Stated plainly in every result.

## 3. Why the framework fits the Passage of Time theme

The passage of time is the medium a life moves through. Two questions capture the whole of how a person relates to that medium: where they face (toward what has been, or toward what will be) and how they move (as one continuous thread, or as a fluid unfolding). Axis 1 measures the first; Axis 2 measures the second. Every other candidate construct collapses into one of these. Mono/polychronic collapses into future planning. CFC collapses into decision horizon. Nostalgia collapses into past engagement. Meaning and wisdom measure something else entirely. The two axes are the irreducible skeleton of "relating to time passing."

The test's framing: *Time is the medium your life moves through. Where do you face, and how do you move?*

## 4. The dimensions measured

Two continuous axes, each 0 to 100.

**Axis 1, Temporal Direction** (where your attention sits across time).
- Construct basis: Zimbardo & Boyd (1999) ZTPI; Sircova et al. (2014); Stolarski & Matthews (2016).
- High pole, Future: oriented toward goals, possibility, what will be. You are pulled forward.
- Low pole, Past: anchored in roots, memory, heritage, what has been. You are grounded backward.
- Scoring: A1 (Past Engagement) is reverse-keyed; A2 (Future Engagement) is normal-keyed. Axis 1 = mean of the normalized A1 and A2 scores. 0 = Past, 100 = Future.
- Honesty notes: see §2.5 (1, 2, 3, 6).

**Axis 2, Temporal Mode** (how your self moves through time).
- Construct basis: Hershfield et al. (2009); Hershfield (2011); McAdams et al. (2001); Adler et al. (2015).
- High pole, Continuous: you experience yourself as one continuous being and your life as one coherent story.
- Low pole, Fluid: your self shifts across time and your life reads as distinct, revisable seasons.
- Scoring: B1 and B2 both normal-keyed. Axis 2 = mean of B1 and B2. 0 = Fluid, 100 = Continuous.
- Honesty notes: see §2.5 (4, 5).

Neither pole of either axis is superior. The person is always a point on the plane, never forced into a binary.

## 5. The four result types

| | **Continuous** (Axis 2 high) | **Fluid** (Axis 2 low) |
|---|---|---|
| **Past** (Axis 1 low) | 📜 The Archivist | 🗣️ The Storyteller |
| **Future** (Axis 1 high) | 🧭 The Navigator | 🌱 The Pioneer |

Full 14-section profiles live in `04b-ptoa-result-profiles.md`.

One-line essences:
- 📜 **The Archivist** (Past + Continuous). You hold your roots as one stable thread and your life as a single, ordered story. You preserve, and you keep.
- 🧭 **The Navigator** (Future + Continuous). You chart one continuous course forward, carrying a coherent sense of where you are headed. You plan, and you pursue.
- 🗣️ **The Storyteller** (Past + Fluid). You weave the past into a living, revisable present, holding memory loosely and retelling as you grow. You remember, and you remake.
- 🌱 **The Pioneer** (Future + Fluid). You move into the new on many emerging threads, comfortable letting the self reshape as the future unfolds. You explore, and you become.

**The Horizon** is the boundary condition (near-center). Named for the present edge where past and future meet. Present-oriented respondents (low on both past and future engagement) and genuinely balanced respondents land here. The Horizon is affirming by design: it reads as living fully in the now, the place where past and future resolve into a single moment. When facet tension fires (two facets on one axis pulling in opposite directions), the modifier rewrites the narrative from "balanced" to "holding opposites."

## 6. Methodology and scoring

Inherits Test 1's model. The math is the same for any two-axis instrument.

**Step 1, collect.** 32 responses, each 1 to 7. Four facets (A1, A2, B1, B2), 8 items each.

**Step 2, direction-normalize.** Map each item so a higher value always means more Future (Axis 1) or more Continuous (Axis 2). A1 items are reverse-keyed (higher past engagement means lower Axis 1). A2, B1, and B2 are normal-keyed.

**Step 3, facet scores.** A1 = mean of normalized A1 items, scaled 0–100. Similarly for A2, B1, B2. The A1 and A2 facet scores are reported in their own direction (A1 high = Past engagement; A2 high = Future engagement) so the result reads naturally.

**Step 4, axis scores.**
- Axis 1 (Temporal Direction) = mean(100 − A1, A2), 0–100. 0 = Past, 100 = Future.
- Axis 2 (Temporal Mode) = mean(B1, B2), 0–100. 0 = Fluid, 100 = Continuous.

**Step 5, locate on the plane.** Center is (50, 50). Quadrant from the signs of Axis1 − 50 and Axis2 − 50.
- Axis1 < 50, Axis2 > 50: The Archivist
- Axis1 > 50, Axis2 > 50: The Navigator
- Axis1 < 50, Axis2 < 50: The Storyteller
- Axis1 > 50, Axis2 < 50: The Pioneer

Prototypicality, angle, gradation, and the boundary classification follow Test 1's v0.19 model exactly. Facet-tension detection is construct-specific and asymmetric across the two axes:
- **Axis 1 (A1 past, A2 future, opposite poles by design).** The SSA "opposite signs" rule does not apply, because the two facets are defined as opposite poles. The PTA instead flags **temporal fullness**: both A1 and A2 strongly engaged (both ≥ 50 + margin). This is the mid-axis-from-fullness case (a person intensely engaged with both past and future), not moderation. It does **not** fire for present-focused respondents (both A1 and A2 low), who read as the affirming Horizon without a tension modifier.
- **Axis 2 (B1, B2, same pole: continuous).** SSA-style opposite-signs tension applies: the two facets pull in opposite directions and both are strong (one continuity-driver, one fluidity-driver), canceling the axis mean.
This asymmetry is verified in the simulation (`ptoa-simulate.ts`): the Fullness case fires Axis 1 tension; the Present case, also at exact center, does not.

**Step 6, classify.**
- r ≥ 0.28: report primary mode plus gradation plus leaning, plus the facet signature. If an axis is tense, the mode is still reported but carries a facet-tension modifier.
- r < 0.28 and no tense axis: report **The Horizon**, the boundary condition.
- r < 0.28 with at least one tense axis: same Horizon, but with the facet-tension modifier rewriting the narrative from "balanced" to "holding opposites."

## 7. Question format and response scale

Format, response scale, and rationale are identical to Test 1. Summarized here; see `01-solstice-cycle-assessment.md` §7.

The format: each question presents a short scenario and two opposing anchor statements on a seven-point scale. The poles alternate randomly so you cannot default to one side. The bipolar format itself handles acquiescence bias.

## 8. Item bank, facets, and lengths

**Two lengths, one item bank.** Standard form: 32 questions (~8–10 minutes). Quick form: 16 questions (~4–5 minutes). Both use the same scoring and give the same result types. The 16 is a subset of the 32: the same items, fewer of them. The 16-item version is a screening-tier quick read (4 items per facet, Spearman-Brown α ≈ .60–.70).

**Facet structure.**
- Axis 1, Temporal Direction: **A1 Past Engagement** (warm, rooted relationship with where you have been) and **A2 Future Engagement** (anticipatory, goal-directed relationship with what will be). Both grounded in ZTPI factors (§2.1). The combination into one axis is a designer choice: see §2.5 honesty note 1.
- Axis 2, Temporal Mode: **B1 Temporal Self-Continuity** (felt connection between your present, past, and future selves; Hershfield) and **B2 Narrative Coherence** (holding your life as one intelligible story; McAdams/Adler adaptation). B2 is a designer facet: see §2.5 honesty note 4.

**Item-writing rules.** Each item varies exactly one facet. No mixing. True opposite poles on one dimension. Neutral language. No transparent giveaways. Gender-neutral and age-agnostic. Both poles affirming. Pole direction randomized at render time.

### Facet A1: Past Engagement (8 items)

All items vary how warmly and meaningfully you relate to your roots, memory, and where you have been. Content drawn from ZTPI Past-Positive and Webster's (1993) adaptive reminiscence functions. No bitterness, regret, or rumination (those are excluded by design: see §2.5 honesty note 2).

| # | Scenario stem | Past pole (rooted) | Future/detached pole |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1.1 | When you think about where you come from… | "I feel a real connection. My roots are part of me." | "I don't feel it much. I'm more focused on what's ahead." |
| A1.2 | Memories of meaningful moments from earlier in your life… | "Are a source of warmth and grounding for me." | "Don't pull at me much. I'm not very sentimental." |
| A1.3 | Traditions, customs, or rituals you grew up with… | "Carry meaning for me. I want to keep them alive." | "Aren't a big part of how I live. I prefer what's fresh." |
| A1.4 | When you think about the people and places that shaped you… | "I feel gratitude and connection." | "I feel some distance. I've moved on to other things." |
| A1.5 | Looking back at how you became who you are… | "I see a clear thread. My history runs through me." | "I see closed chapters. Where I'm going matters more." |
| A1.6 | When a memory from your past surfaces… | "I can sit with it easily. It feels like home." | "I tend to look forward instead. The past is past." |
| A1.7 | The stories, music, or art from an earlier time that you grew up with… | "Still move me. They're part of who I am." | "Feel distant from who I am now." |
| A1.8 | How much of your sense of self comes from your roots and where you've been? | "A lot. I'm grounded in my history." | "Not much. I'm shaped more by where I'm headed." |

### Facet A2: Future Engagement (8 items)

All items vary how much you are pulled toward goals, possibility, and what will be. Content drawn from ZTPI Future and the Future-Positive factor. No Future-Negative (anxious dread): see §2.5 honesty note 2.

| # | Scenario stem | Future pole (forward) | Present/grounded pole |
|---|---|---|---|
| A2.1 | When you think about the years ahead… | "I feel pulled toward them. There's so much I want to build." | "I take them as they come. I'm not chasing a far-off horizon." |
| A2.2 | Goals and plans for the future… | "Are essential to me. I'm always working toward something." | "Take a back seat. I prefer to live in the present." |
| A2.3 | Imagining who you could become… | "Feels vivid and motivating. The future pulls me forward." | "Feels distant. I'd rather be fully here than dwell on what's next." |
| A2.4 | When you make choices… | "I think a lot about where they'll lead. The future matters." | "I weigh what's right for now. The distant future isn't my compass." |
| A2.5 | Hopes and dreams for what's ahead… | "Shape a lot of how I live day to day." | "Don't drive me much. I'm content with the present." |
| A2.6 | The sense that your best experiences might still be ahead… | "Excites me. I'm building toward them." | "Doesn't pull me. I find meaning in the present." |
| A2.7 | How much of your energy goes into what's coming? | "A great deal. I'm oriented toward the horizon." | "Very little. My attention is here, now." |
| A2.8 | When someone asks what you're working toward… | "I can talk about it for hours. The future is alive for me." | "I'm not sure what to say. I live more by the day." |

### Facet B1: Temporal Self-Continuity (8 items)

All items vary the felt connection between your present self and your past and future selves. Grounded in Hershfield et al. (2009) similarity, connectedness, caring, and liking, extended to both temporal directions.

| # | Scenario stem | Continuous pole (connected) | Distinct pole (separate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1.1 | The person you were ten years ago and the person you are now… | "Feel like the same core self, grown." | "Feel like quite different people." |
| B1.2 | When you imagine yourself ten years from now… | "I feel a strong connection to that future self." | "They feel distant, almost like a stranger." |
| B1.3 | How much do you care about your future self's well-being? | "Deeply. They're me, continuing." | "It feels abstract. I focus on who I am now." |
| B1.4 | Looking back, the decisions your past self made… | "Still feel like mine. I see the throughline." | "Feel like someone else's choices. I've moved on." |
| B1.5 | When you make a choice today… | "I weigh how my future self will feel about it." | "I trust whoever I become to handle things their own way." |
| B1.6 | The version of you from years ago that you still recognize… | "Is unmistakably still here. I've always been me." | "Has faded. I feel rebuilt across the years." |
| B1.7 | Across the seasons of your life, your sense of who you are… | "Holds steady. There's a clear thread." | "Shifts a lot. I become different people over time." |
| B1.8 | If you met your past self, you'd feel… | "A real kinship. I know them." | "Like meeting someone I once knew but no longer am." |

### Facet B2: Narrative Coherence (8 items)

All items vary whether you hold your life as one intelligible, meaningful story or as distinct, self-standing seasons. Self-report adaptation of McAdams et al. (2001) and Adler et al. (2015). Designer facet: see §2.5 honesty note 4.

| # | Scenario stem | Coherent pole (one story) | Episodic pole (distinct seasons) |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2.1 | The story of your life so far… | "Has a clear shape. I can see how it all fits." | "Is more a series of chapters than one plot." |
| B2.2 | When you explain how you got to where you are… | "There's a thread I can trace from beginning to now." | "It's a collection of turns. I don't see one storyline." |
| B2.3 | The events of your life, even the hard ones… | "Fit into a larger meaning I can name." | "Stand on their own. I don't force them into a story." |
| B2.4 | Looking for patterns across your years… | "Comes naturally. I see what ties them together." | "Feels artificial. Each time was its own thing." |
| B2.5 | When something difficult happened… | "It eventually wove into a larger meaning in my life." | "It lived as its own moment. Not every chapter has to resolve." |
| B2.6 | Your sense of your life's direction… | "Forms a coherent arc I can describe." | "Is open. I let each season be what it is." |
| B2.7 | When you think about who you're becoming… | "It's the next chapter of an ongoing story I know well." | "It's an open page. The next season may take any shape." |

A launch pass should run an internal-consistency check per facet and drop or replace the weakest 1 to 2 items if any facet alpha falls below .75. B2 is the highest-risk facet (designer-adapted) and should be watched first.

**Overlap honesty.** Axis 1 shares substantial variance with Conscientiousness (Future ↔ Conscientiousness r = .60, Stolarski & Matthews 2016) and therefore with Test 2's deliberate pole. Axis 2's B1 shares conceptual space with Test 3's identity domain. These are validated constructs the PTA builds on. The PTA's contribution is the temporal framing and the facet combination, not trait dimensions independent of personality.

## 9. Interpretation and reporting

A result has four layers, surfaced in order:
1. **Your mode** (quadrant). The shareable label.
2. **Your position** (angle). Gradation and secondary leaning.
3. **Your prototypicality** (radius). How strongly you fit your mode. Near-center yields **The Horizon**, the boundary condition.
4. **Your narrative.** Two to three personalized paragraphs, ending with a "your reflection point" line. Gemini layer per the GDD.

**Deterministic fallback (no-API path).** 12 seasonal-mode states (4 modes × {early, mid, late}) plus the Horizon boundary reading (two narrative variants via the facet-tension modifier). The type count is four; the Horizon is a boundary condition, not a fifth type. The game is fully playable without the API.

**Never reported as:** a maturity score, an intelligence score, a diagnostic, a fixed destiny, or a clinical assessment. Always framed as a snapshot of how you currently relate to time passing.

## 10. Strengths, limitations, intended use

**Strengths.**
- Dimensional underneath, archetypal on top. Inherits Test 1's structural honesty.
- Two axes measuring distinct aspects of relating to time: where you face (past or future) and how you move (continuous or fluid).
- Axis 1 grounded in the most-validated temporal construct in the literature (ZTPI), confirmed across 24 countries.
- Axis 2 grounded in temporal self-continuity (Hershfield), a clean, validated, self-report construct with documented behavioral consequences, plus a self-report adaptation of the consequential narrative-identity tradition.
- Theme resonance is structural: the test measures how you move through time as a self, not how you schedule tasks.
- Affirming by design. No bad mode. Past-anchored reframed as rootedness, not being stuck. Fluid reframed as freedom and presence, not incoherence. Future reframed as vision, not anxiety. Present-oriented (the boundary) reframed as living in the now, not as a gap.
- Replaces a weaker proposed axis (mono/polychronic) with a defensible construct (self-continuity and narrative coherence), preserving the original archetype names.

**Limitations.**
- Not a validated clinical instrument. Inspired by validated models. Not independently psychometrically evaluated.
- Self-report biases remain. Temporal constructs may be susceptible to present-state effects: a person's time perspective shifts with life circumstances, age, and endings (Carstensen, 2021). The PTA captures current disposition, not a pure trait.
- Axis 1 composites two separable ZTPI factors and excludes the negative-valence past (§2.5 honesty notes 1 and 2). The axis score is a directional summary, not a claim that past and future are a single bipolar trait, and not a measure of rumination or regret.
- The ZTPI has no validated positive-present factor (Vowinckel et al., 2015). Present orientation is handled as an affirming boundary condition, not as a validated axis pole.
- B2 Narrative Coherence is a self-report adaptation of an interview-coded construct (§2.5 honesty note 4). It is a designer facet, not a validated instrument.
- Axis 1 shares substantial variance with Conscientiousness (Stolarski & Matthews, 2016) and therefore with Test 2. B1 shares conceptual space with Test 3's identity domain. The cross-suite overlap is real and stated, not eliminable.
- State versus trait ambiguity. Time perspective and self-continuity both shift with major transitions, aging, and perceived endings (Carstensen, 2021). The PTA is a snapshot of current temporal orientation.

**Intended use.** Reflective self-discovery and play within the Solstice Soul game. Conversation-starting, shareable content about how you relate to time.

**Explicitly not intended for.** Clinical diagnosis, employment decisions, retirement planning, relationship matching, or any high-stakes real-world application.

A short, plain-language disclaimer appears with every result.
