                                                                   Abstract The ten-item Inventory of Polychronic Values (IPV), a psychometric measure of
                                                                   polychronicity (the extent to which people in a culture prefer to be engaged in two or more tasks or
                                                                   events simultaneously and believe their preference is the best way to do things), was developed
                                                                   using data from 11 samples (N = 2,190) collected from bank employees, undergraduate students,
                                                                   hospital personnel, dentists and their staffs, and state agency managers. Principal components,
                                                                   alpha, correlation, and confirmatory factor analyses supported the IPV in its internal consistency,
                                                                   test-retest reliability, content adequacy, construct validity (both discriminant and convergent), and
                                                                   nomological validity.

                                                                   Polychronicity
                                                                   After first introducing the concept (Hall, 1959), Hall (1983, pp. 45-46)
                                                                   subsequently described polychronicity as a cultural variable involving two
                                                                   different ways of organizing activities:
                                                                     (1) monochronically ± involvement in events one event at a time; and
                                                                     (2) polychronically ± involvement in two or more events at the same time;
                                                                   the monochronic and polychronic approaches being opposites (Hall and Hall,
                                                                   1990, p. 13).
                                                                      As a cultural variable then, we define polychronicity as the extent to which
                                                                   people in a culture:
                                                                     (1) prefer to be engaged in two or more tasks or events simultaneously; and
                                                                     (2) believe their preference is the best way to do things.
                                                                   This definition taps both the values (``preferences'') and beliefs (``believe ...'')
                                                                   attributes included in many definitions of culture (see Kroeber and Kluckhohn,
                                                                   1952). Bluedorn et al. (1992) have noted that the element of ``at the same time'' or
                                                                   ``simultaneously'' in the polychronicity concept can refer literally to ``at the
                                                                   same time'', such as an individual eating lunch and reading a newspaper
                                                                   simultaneously, as well as to the active interspersing and dovetailing of several
                                                                   activities within the same time period, such as a morning's work. In extremely
                                                                   monochronic cultures, people would tend to focus on a single task or project for
                                                                   the entire morning and regard unscheduled events (e.g. phone calls) as
                                                                   interruptions. Conversely, people in more polychronic cultures would
                                                                   anticipate involvement with multiple activities during the same morning
                                                                   period, intending to move back and forth among several tasks and projects
                                                                   during the morning. Unscheduled events such as phone calls, or colleagues and
                                                                   Journal of   customers dropping in without appointments, would be interpreted as part of
                                                                   Managerial   the morning's normal activities rather than as interruptions or deviations from
                                                                   Psychology   the plan or schedule[1].

                                                                                Sample 1

                                                                                (1991) scale and developed two completely new items. We included these six

                                                                                members of the executive committee. This 205-respondent sample had a mean

                                                                                different hierarchical levels as follows: middle and top managers, 16.1 percent;
                                                                                supervisory-level managers, 15.1 percent; and non-managerial personnel,

                                                                                the proposed six items loaded on a single factor (all five loadings were  0.60)

                                                                                the five items produced an alpha coefficient (Cronbach, 1951) of 0.74, which was

                                                                                PAI. However, Lautenschlager's (1989) ALPHATST program revealed this

                                                                   Assessing the IPV's content adequacy (validity)[2]
                                                                   The Q-method approach
                                                                   Schriesheim et al. (1993) defined content adequacy as the degree to which a
                                                                   measure's items are a proper sample of the theoretical content domain of a
                                                                   construct, and we have followed their recommendation to use the term
                                                                   ``adequacy'' rather than ``validity'' due to usage criticisms of the latter in this
                                                                   context. Further, they emphasized the strategic importance of a measure
                                                                   containing a representative sample of the underlying construct's theoretical
                                                                   universe, perhaps even in correct proportion to a construct's subdomains. They
                                                                   note that content adequacy has historically been assessed subjectively rather
                                                                   than through quantitative analytical procedures. To provide a quantitative
                                                                   approach, Schriesheim et al. (1993) developed the Q-method approach based on
                                                                   Stephenson's (1953) Q-methodology. This is the approach to content-adequacy
                                                                   assessment that we employed.
                                                                      The technique requires judges to complete a questionnaire containing two or
                                                                   more scales or subscales. The first scale's definition is presented to the judges
                                                                   who are then asked to rate each item according to how much it refers to the
                                                                   construct whose definition was presented. Each item from the intended scale as
                                                                   well as from the other scales is assessed this way. The process is then repeated
                                                                   with the definitions of each of the other scales, so each judge evaluates each
                                                                   individual scale item as many times as there are scale definitions ± and then
                                                                   once more because the final portion of the questionnaire asks each judge to rate
                                                                   each item according to how much it refers to something other than any of the
                                                                   Journal of   definitions provided for the preceding ratings. Results of these ratings are then
                                                                   Managerial   compared. A detailed description of this method is provided in Schriesheim
                                                                                et al. (1993).

                                                                                A known-groups test
                                                                                A known-groups test for content adequacy compares scale scores for groups
                                                                                whose levels on the variable of interest are already known by other means. If
                                                                                the scale successfully differentiates the groups in the manner already known, it
                                                                                demonstrates content adequacy (Kerlinger, 1973, p. 495). To perform a known-
                                                                                groups test of the IPV, we needed to measure the polychronicity levels of
                                                                                different organizations with some instrument other than the IPV, then apply
                                                                                the IPV to those same organizations and compare the two sets of scores. A high
                                                                                level of similarity would indicate strong support for the IPV's content adequacy
                                                                                whereas a small or non-existent degree of similarity would indicate a lack of
                                                                                content adequacy.
                                                                                   The difficulty of performing such a test on the IPV is the absence of any
                                                                                other psychometric measures of polychronicity as a cultural attribute. Indeed,
                                                                                their absence is the reason we decided to develop such a measure. Hall's
                                                                                previous empirical research on polychronicity in national cultures, for example,
                                                                                was all based on qualitative methods and observations. To undertake
                                                                                comparable qualitative measurements to obtain judgments of the
                                                                                polychronicity levels in a large enough sample of organizations (i.e. N = 30-50)
                                                                                to provide a meaningful correlation with IPV scores from the same
                                                                                organizations would require years, perhaps a lifetime to produce.
                                                                                Consequently, we approached the problem by creating organizations with
                                                                                known polychronicity levels.
                                                                                   The ``organizations'' we created consisted of two descriptions of managerial
                                                                                behaviors and attitudes in organizations, one describing a very polychronic
                                                                                organization, the other a very monochronic organization. Care was taken to
                                                                                avoid any of the phrasing from the ten IPV items or even comparably
                                                                                synonymous phrasing in these descriptions so that the application of the IPV to
                                                                                these scenarios would provide a meaningful known-groups content adequacy
                                                                                test. In other words, we avoided statements in the scenarios such as ``Jane is
                                                                                working on several projects at the same time'' because a respondent applying
                                                                                the IPV to a scenario with such statements would simply be identifying scale

                                                                                Nomological validity
                                                                                Cronbach and Meehl (1955) proposed testing a nomological net to support




Downloaded by UNIVERSITY OF OTAGO At 15:12 12 November 2016 (PT)
                                                                                claims for a scale's construct validity, a nomological net being the interlocking
                                                                                system of laws constituting a theory. They also noted that in a construct's early
                                                                                history, the network will be limited and the construct will have few
                                                                                connections. Although polychronicity can properly be described as a construct
                                                                                early in its history, Hall's work began a nomological net that provides
                                                                                predictions about the relationships among polychronicity, punctuality, and
                                                                                schedules-and-deadlines values at the cultural level of analysis.
                                                                                   The observations by Hall (1983) and Hall and Hall (1990) indicate
                                                                                polychronicity values should be negatively correlated with both punctuality
                                                                                and schedules-and-deadlines values. Specifically, the more polychronic the
                                                                                culture, the less punctuality will be valued and the less importance will be
                                                                                attached to meeting schedules and deadlines. Completing this small
                                                                                nomological net, punctuality values and schedules-and-deadlines values should
                                                                                be positively correlated.
                                                                                   We tested this nomological net by calculating the correlations among the
                                                                                temporal values of the departments in the two hospitals (samples 10 and 11).
                                                                                Respondents in these hospitals had also completed the IPV and Schriber and
                                                                                Gutek's (1987) punctuality and schedules-and-deadlines scales with their
                                                                                departments as the referent. Hence, in the analysis of this nomological net the
                                                                                respondent's department was the referent rather than the respondent's entire
                                                                                organization, and each department's score for each temporal value was the
                                                                                mean of the respondents from that department. The correlations among the
                                                                                three variables are presented in Table VII, all of which are significant, and
                                                                                more importantly, in the expected directions: punctuality and schedules-and-
                                                                                deadlines values were positively correlated, and both punctuality values and
                                                                                schedules-and-deadlines values were negatively correlated with polychronicity.
                                                                                   When examining group cultures ± departmental cultures in this case ± an
                                                                                important issue involves how many respondents or judges are required to
                                                                                make observations about each group (department). Because some of the data
                                                                                upon which the correlations in Table VII are based came from single observers
                                                                                (sometimes only one individual was included from very small departments), the
                                                                                correlation matrix in Table VII was recalculated nine times with increasing
                                                                                requirements for the number of respondents per unit. As this requirement
                                                                                increased from at least one respondent per unit to at least ten respondents per
                                                                   department, the number of departments in the analysis declined steadily (e.g.                   Inventory of

                                                                   Discussion and conclusion
                                                                   Over a development process spanning 11 samples that totaled 2,190
                                                                   respondents, the Inventory of Polychronic Values was supported as a valid and
                                                                   reliable measure of polychronicity. In addition to meeting the goal of
                                                                   consistently producing alpha reliability coefficients in the 0.80 range (the
                                                                   median of the 13 alphas is 0.84), the IPV demonstrated:
                                                                      (1) strong test-retest reliability;
                                                                      (2) little, if any, susceptibility to social desirability response biases;
                                                                      (3) content adequacy in an extremely supportive set of results based on
                                                                          Schriesheim et al.'s (1993) Q-Method and an equally supportive known
                                                                          groups test;
                                                                      (4) stable theoretical homogeneity in a series of confirmatory factor
                                                                          analyses conducted in and across three large samples;
                                                                      (5) discriminant validity by achieving a better model fit in two samples
                                                                          when scales measuring punctuality and schedules-and-deadlines values
                                                                          were included in CFAs comparing one-, two-, and three-factor models of
                                                                          the factor structure; and
                                                                      (6) convergent validity by all ten IPV items loading significantly on a single
                                                                          polychronicity factor in all of the confirmatory factor analyses conducted.
